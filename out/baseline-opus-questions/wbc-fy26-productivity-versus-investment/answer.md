# Westpac FY26: does >$500m of productivity restore positive jaws?

**Judgment: it is mainly an offset.** The targeted FY26 productivity is about the same
size as the FY26 cost pressures Westpac itself identifies. Reported jaws can still turn
positive in FY26, but the swing factor is the $273m restructuring charge dropping out of
the base, not productivity outrunning investment.

## The FY25 bridge sets the base

| FY25 expense bridge ($m) | |
|---|---:|
| FY24 | 10,944 |
| Staff costs | +397 |
| Technology | +146 |
| Volume and other | +199 |
| Productivity | (402) |
| Investments | +359 |
| Restructuring charge | +273 |
| FY25 | 11,916 |

FY25 productivity of $402m covered only 37% of the $1,101m of gross pressure. Income rose
3% ($21,763m to $22,464m) against 9% expense growth. Jaws were about -5.7ppts, or about
-3.2ppts excluding the restructuring charge. The cost to income ratio ex Notable Items
went from 50.3% to 53.0%.

## The FY26 pressures against >$500m

- **Wages.** The EBA increase is 3% to 4% on a $6,326m staff expense base, plus a
  superannuation increase.
- **Bankers.** About 610 announced front line roles (~350 Business, ~80 WIB, ~180
  Consumer) were only c.40% to c.60% onboarded at Sep-25. A full year cost still arrives.
- **UNITE.** Spend rises to $850m-$950m at ~75% expensed, so roughly $638m-$713m goes
  through the P&L, against roughly $488m-$502m in FY25 (our arithmetic).
- **Amortisation.** It rose $106m in FY25 to $995m. FY26 growth is guided only to
  "moderate", not to stop.

The >$500m target is only about $100m above the FY25 productivity of $402m. The listed
pressures are of similar order. The target therefore stabilises the run-rate; it does not
create headroom.

## What actually creates positive jaws in FY26

Two effects sit outside productivity.

1. **The restructuring charge.** $273m is 2.3% of the FY25 expense base. Its non-repeat
   alone lowers FY26 reported expense growth by that amount.
2. **The investment mix, which cuts the other way.** Total spend stays at ~$2bn, with
   growth & productivity and risk & regulatory both falling. But UNITE is ~75% expensed
   against 60% for total spend in FY25, so a flat envelope pushes more cost into the P&L.

The UNITE step-up does decelerate. FY25 added $399m of UNITE expense; FY26 adds roughly
$140m-$225m (our arithmetic). But UNITE runs at c.40% of a c.$2bn p.a. envelope in
FY27-FY28 and the plan extends into FY29, so the drag persists well past FY26.

**Conclusion.** On this disclosure the productivity target is an offset to wage, banker
and UNITE pressure. Positive underlying jaws in FY26 require revenue growth above roughly
1% to 2%, and Westpac gives no FY26 revenue guidance.

One more sign that the target is an offset: average FTE still rose 1% to 35,678 in FY25,
because UNITE and banker hiring more than offset productivity reductions.

## Limitations

- No FY26 income or revenue guidance, so jaws cannot be computed. Only 1H26 NIM
  sensitivities are given.
- Westpac does not say whether a further restructuring charge occurs in FY26, nor does it
  disclose the total Fit for Growth cost, FTE impact or benefit profile.
- ">$500m targeted productivity" is not defined as gross or net, and FY25's $402m is not
  stated to be on the same basis.
- UNITE cost-out benefits are not quantified anywhere. No dollar benefit or timing.
- The two documents disagree on the FY25 UNITE expensed share: 74% (announcement, p19)
  against 76% (presentation, p15). We prefer 74%; the $13m gap changes nothing.
- "c.40% of total investment spend FY27 - FY28" is ambiguous. With its footnote it reads
  as UNITE being ~40% of a c.$2bn p.a. envelope, but it could mean 40% of the program.
- The split of UNITE cost between the bridge's "Staff costs" and "Investments" bars is not
  disclosed. Staff expenses are separately said to include UNITE.
- The FY26 UNITE expensed step-up is our arithmetic, not a disclosed number.
- Segment expense growth rates are distorted by compositional changes, which Westpac did
  not restate in FY24 comparatives.
