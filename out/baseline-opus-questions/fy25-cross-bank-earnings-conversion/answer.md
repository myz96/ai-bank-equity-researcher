# FY25 earnings conversion: CBA best, then NAB, then Westpac

**Judgment.** CBA converted best. It is the only one of the three whose preferred measure
grew at all, and it leads on both steps of the conversion chain. Westpac lost the result at
the cost line. NAB lost it below the pre-provision line.

## The chain, on each bank's own preferred basis, FY25 v FY24

| Line | CBA cash, FY to Jun-25 [1] | NAB cash, FY to Sep-25 [5] | WBC ex-Notables, FY to Sep-25 [9] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating income | 28,465, +5% | 20,813, +2.9% | 22,464, +3% |
| Operating expenses | (12,996), +6% | (9,848), +4.6% | (11,916), +9% |
| Pre-provision line | 15,469, +3% | 10,965, +1.3% | 10,548, (3%) |
| Impairment | (726), (9%) | (833), +14.4% | (424), (21%) |
| Preferred earnings | 10,252, +4% | 7,091, (0.2%) | 6,972, (2%) |
| Cost-income ratio | 45.7% from 45.0% [4] | 47.3% from 46.5% [8] | 53.0% from 50.3% [10] |

Two gaps, from my arithmetic on those printed lines. Income growth less expense growth: CBA
-1.6pp, NAB -1.8pp, WBC -5.7pp. Pre-provision growth to earnings growth: CBA +0.8pp, WBC
+0.5pp, NAB -1.5pp. CBA leads both steps. Second place turns on which step you weight.

## Drivers

**CBA.** Expenses (+6%) outran income (+5%), so operating performance rose only 3%. A $76m
fall in loan impairment expense then lifted cash NPAT to +4% [3]. Its income was also the
most core: NIM +9bps to 2.08%, +2bps after liquid assets and pooled facilities [2][4].

**Westpac.** Expenses rose 9%, and still 6% without a $273m Fit for Growth restructuring
charge that Westpac did not classify as a Notable Item [10][11]. Pre-provision profit fell
3%; adding the charge back leaves it flat, my arithmetic, not a disclosed line. The largest
credit tailwind of the three, 5bps of average loans against 7bps, recovered only half a point
[9][11]. Core NIM slipped 1bp to 1.81% [12].

**NAB.** NAB had the tightest cost growth, +4.6%, or +3.2% without $130m of payroll review
and remediation cost, and delivered +1.3% underlying profit [6]. It was then the only bank
with a rising credit charge, $833m or +14.4%, from individually assessed charges in Corporate
and Institutional Banking [7], plus non-controlling interests of $39m against $18m [5]. Its
revenue was also the softest: +1.4% excluding Markets and Treasury, not +2.9% [6].

## Comparability limits

- Different periods. CBA's year ends 30 June 2025, NAB's and Westpac's on 30 September 2025.
  No document restates to a common period.
- Different adjustment scope. Westpac excludes only hedging items, $56m after tax [13]. CBA
  also excludes disposal, closure and demerger results, $137m [2]. NAB also excludes
  amortisation of acquired intangibles, $303m, of which $295m is acquisitions and disposals
  [5][14][15]. NAB's bar is the lowest to clear and Westpac's the highest.
- One-off costs stay inside all three pre-provision lines, $273m at WBC and $130m at each of
  CBA and NAB. None of the three publishes a full-year pre-provision profit without them.
- Statutory profit ranks them differently: CBA +8%, WBC -1%, NAB -2.9% [1][9][5], so NAB and
  Westpac swap. I prefer the preferred-measure comparison, because the question is each bank's
  own conversion chain, but the swap shows the ordering depends on adjustment scope. CBA's two
  bases disagree on its own pre-provision step: +5% statutory against +3% cash [1].
- The documents give no normalised credit charge and no mix-adjusted comparison, so the credit
  swings cannot be separated from portfolio quality.
