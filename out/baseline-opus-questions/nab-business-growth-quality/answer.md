# NAB FY25 Business & Private Banking — was the growth value-accretive?

**Judgment: broadly yes, but the incremental dollar earned less.** B&PB grew volume across
sectors, did not buy 2H volume with price, and its business-lending credit charge actually
fell. What it gave up was operating leverage and capital efficiency, not underwriting
discipline.

## 1. The reconciliation (FY25 v FY24)

| Line | FY25 | Move |
|---|---|---|
| GLAs / business lending | $279.5bn / $166.3bn | +6.8% / +7.3% |
| Average interest earning assets | $249.0bn | +5.4% |
| Net interest income | $7,499m | +2.8% |
| Net operating income | $8,531m | +2.1% |
| Operating expenses | $3,232m | +2.9% |
| Credit impairment charge | $529m | +1.1% |
| Cash earnings | $3,330m | +1.6% |
| Risk-weighted assets | $159.2bn | +9.0% |

Cash earnings rose $53m: NII +$204m, other income −$31m, costs −$91m, credit −$6m, tax −$23m.
6.8% of volume became 1.6% of earnings through three leaks — NIM −8bps (3.09%→3.01%), negative
jaws (cost-to-income 37.6%→37.9%), and RWA growth outrunning GLA growth. Return on average assets
fell 5bps to 1.25%; return on **average RWA was flat at 2.18%**. The new book earned roughly the
average risk-adjusted return but consumed more capital per dollar lent.

## 2. Margin: a real cost, but not the price of 2H volume

The 8bps is attributed to competition in *both* business and housing lending *and* to higher
deposit costs and mix — deposit cost is not a price paid for loan growth. The second-half test
is decisive: GLAs +4.5% HoH while NIM rose 1bp to 3.02%, and the Group NIM bridge shows lending
margin flat with Australian business lending only −1bp. B&PB's 3.01% NIM remains 1.7x the Group's
1.74% and the highest of any division, and APRA business-lending share rose 21.5%→21.7%.

## 3. Credit: the charge exonerates, the stock warns

**P&L:** B&PB's *business lending* charge **fell** from $517m to $431m; the divisional charge rose
only because home lending swung from −$7m to +$53m. Charge/GLAs improved 1bp to 0.19%. Mix barely
moved (sub-investment-grade 78%→77%, fully secured 74%→73%, PD≥2.5% 14% at Mar-25 and Sep-25).

**Balance sheet:** B&PB NPEs rose 26.9% to $7,079m (2.12%→2.54% of GLAs) — four times lending
growth. Business-lending NPLs reached 3.22% of business GLAs (impaired 0.60%→0.73%), the highest
in the series disclosed back to Sep-16, and 2H25 charges jumped to $292m (0.21% annualised).

## 4. Broad-based versus single-name

**Growth is broad-based:** Other +10.4% ($68bn), Small business +9.2% ($13bn), CRE +7.4% ($42bn),
Health +5.9% ($10bn), Agri +3.5% ($46bn).

**Deterioration is not co-located with growth.** The Group's *impaired* step-up is single names in
C&IB and NZ Banking, not B&PB. B&PB's contribution is the default-but-not-impaired ratio — and the
2H25 move there is explicitly "a small number of large customers in the Agriculture portfolio",
narrowed elsewhere to "two large well secured customers". Australian agriculture NPE/EAD jumped
1.99%→2.95%, yet Agri was the *slowest*-growing sector (+3.5%). CRE grew 7.4% with its NPE ratio
up only 5bps YoY (and down HoH). Group watch loans fell 9bps to 1.17%, the first decline since
Mar-23. NAB describes the FY25 default-but-not-impaired rise as portfolio-wide, so the underlying
drift is broad; the visible 2H spike is lumpy, in a low-growth, well-secured book.

**Bottom line:** value-accretive in level (B&PB+C&IB combined ROE 18%), dilutive at the margin.
The binding constraint is capital and cost, not spread or underwriting.
