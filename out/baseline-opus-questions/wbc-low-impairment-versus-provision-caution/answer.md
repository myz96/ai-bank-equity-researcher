# Westpac FY25: the charge normalised, the provision stock did not

**Verdict: both are true, and not in conflict. The FY25 charge is a write-off number that
says little about forward risk. The September 2025 stock still carries material caution
that Westpac increased — but that caution now rests on a scenario weight, not on observed
portfolio stress.**

## 1. The $424m charge is mostly cash losses already incurred

| FY25 charge build ($m) | |
|---|---|
| CAP direct write-offs (cards, personal loans) | (561) |
| Other changes in CAP | 103 |
| **Total CAP** | **(458)** |
| Net IAP (new 408, write-backs 195, recoveries 247) | 34 |
| **Total** | **(424)** = 5bps (FY24: 537, 7bps) |

Remove the $561m write-off run-rate and Westpac ran a **net $137m release** through P&L.
The forward-looking components were a benefit, not a build.

## 2. The balance sheet did not release

Total ECL fell only $109m (-2%) to $4,987m: modelled CAP -$168m, overlays **+$59m**,
IAPs +$3m, debt securities -$3m. Falling ratios (provisions/gross loans 63→58bps;
CAP/credit RWA 130→125bps) are denominator effects — gross loans grew 6% to $856.4bn.

## 3. Caution is measurable, and it rose

- Downside scenario weight **42.5% → 47.5%**; upside halved, 5.0% → 2.5%.
- Reported probability-weighted ECL $4,987m against a 100% base case ECL of **$3,040m**
  and a 100% downside ECL of **$7,152m**. The stock is **$1.9bn (64%) above base case**
  and sits 47% of the way from base to downside — that is, priced at the downside weight.
- The downside is severe: GDP -6%, unemployment 11%, residential property prices -27%,
  commercial property prices -32% (trough/peak values; property is a two-year cumulative
  reduction), against a base case of 4.4-4.5% unemployment.
- Overlays +33% to $238m for "portfolio seasoning and geographical areas experiencing
  higher stress not included in modelled outcomes", funded by releasing the construction,
  Australian mortgage and consumer-finance overlays.
- Stage 2 exposure fell 15.26% → 11.43% of TCE, yet coverage of the remainder **rose**
  (non-stressed 1.10% → 1.21%; stressed 7.94% → 9.24%).

## 4. Where caution actually fell

Impaired coverage dropped 41.28% → 39.53% while impaired exposures rose 3% to $2,013m.
Business & Wealth's charge rose 73% to $245m on new IAPs, overlays and the downside
weight, against Institutional (+$1m) and New Zealand (+NZ$44m) benefits. Idiosyncratic
corporate risk is building as consumer risk fades.

## 5. Quality of the improvement

Mortgage 90+ delinquencies fell 1.05% → 0.70% "due to a reduction in hardship and a
change to serviceability treatment"; other-consumer 90+ fell 1.40% → 1.08% "driven by
the sale of the auto finance portfolio". Part of the headline improvement is portfolio
action and definition, not credit healing.

**Conclusion.** Normalisation is complete in the flow and in the consumer book. It is not
complete in the stock. Westpac holds ~$1.9bn of macro insurance it chose to add to, and
the release optionality is real — but so is the rising individually assessed risk in
Business, where coverage is thinning.
