NAB's Cash ROE fell 20 bps, from 11.6% in FY24 to 11.4% in FY25 (ev-1, ev-5, ev-13). The decline is almost entirely a denominator story: cash earnings were essentially flat at $7,091m versus $7,102m, down $11m or 0.2% (ev-2, ev-8, ev-17, ev-18), while total average equity attributable to owners rose $1,316m from $61,039m to $62,355m (ev-3). NAB publishes no ROE walk chart; the earnings/equity split below is derived arithmetically.

## Limitations
- NAB publishes no ROE walk or bridge chart for FY25 v FY24 in either document, so both contributions are arithmetic derivations from the ROE endpoints and the disclosed cash earnings movement, not bank-disclosed bars. Confidence is capped accordingly.
- The equity_effect of -0.18ppt is a residual (total delta less earnings effect) and therefore absorbs the interaction term; NAB does not separately quantify retained earnings, buyback or DRP contributions to AVERAGE equity.
- The CET1 movement charts in both documents (results book p.30, presentation p.29) cover Mar 25 to Sep 25 — a half-on-half comparison, not the FY25 v FY24 task span — so none of their bars is used as a contribution. They are regulatory capital ratio walks, not equity-dollar walks, in any case.
- The average non-controlling interests line nearly doubled ($394m to $751m), which reduces the ROE denominator; NAB does not explain this movement on the ROE note page.
- NAB's own narrative on p.6 of the results book characterises cash earnings as 'broadly stable', a qualification that means the earnings effect is genuinely near zero rather than a meaningful driver.
