# WBC FY25: Low Impairment Charge vs. Provision Stock Caution

**Verdict: Normalisation is real but not complete.** The low P&L charge is
genuinely earned — realised delinquencies and stressed exposures fell across
every segment. But the balance-sheet provision keeps a deliberate, growing
buffer against downside risk, so "normalisation complete" overstates the
disclosure.

## Charge flow (Group, $m)

| | FY25 | FY24 | 2H25 | 1H25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| New IAPs | (408) | (423) | (157) | (251) |
| Write-backs + recoveries | 442 | 283 | 238 | 204 |
| **Net IAP** | **34** | **(140)** | **81** | **(47)** |
| Write-offs | (561) | (486) | (282) | (279) |
| Other CAP changes | 103 | 89 | 27 | 76 |
| **Net CAP** | **(458)** | **(397)** | **(255)** | **(203)** |
| **Total charge** | **(424)** | **(537)** | **(174)** | **(250)** |
| Charge / avg loans | 5bps | 7bps | 4bps | 6bps |
| Net write-offs / avg loans | 6bps | 5bps | 6bps | 6bps |

The fall in the P&L charge came from write-backs/recoveries almost doubling
and new IAPs shrinking — not from lower realised losses: net write-offs to
average loans actually ticked up 1bp to 6bps.

## Provision stock and scenarios ($m, 30-Sep-25 vs 31-Mar-25 vs 30-Sep-24)

| | Sep-25 | Mar-25 | Sep-24 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Modelled CAP | 4,201 | 4,321 | 4,369 |
| Overlays | 238 | 130 | 179 |
| Total CAP | 4,439 | 4,451 | 4,548 |
| IAP | 539 | 611 | 536 |
| **Total ECL provision** | **4,987** | **5,072** | **5,096** |
| Downside scenario weight | 47.5% | 45.0% | 42.5% |
| Upside scenario weight | 2.5% | 5.0% | 5.0% |

The provision stock fell only 2% in a year of falling delinquencies, because
two offsetting forces ran through it. Improving economics and lower mortgage
arrears cut modelled CAP by $168m over the year, but a 5-percentage-point
swing of scenario weight from upside into downside, plus a $59m rise in
overlays (to $238m, its highest point across the three dates shown), clawed
most of that back. Westpac's own investor pack quantifies the resulting
caution directly: reported probability-weighted ECL of $4,987m sits **$1.9bn
above** a hypothetical 100%-base-case ECL of $3,040m, though still well below
a 100%-downside ECL of $7,152m.

Coverage ratios are drifting down (CAP/credit RWA 125bps vs 130bps a year
ago; total provisions/gross loans 58bps vs 63bps), so the buffer is thinning
at the margin even as its downside-weighting rises — a mixed signal, not a
clean "caution intact" story either.

## Conclusion

The low charge reflects real, broad-based credit improvement (stressed
exposures to TCE 1.28% vs 1.45%; mortgage 90+ delinquencies 0.70% vs 1.05%).
But management chose to raise the downside scenario weight and overlays
rather than let the full economic improvement flow through to the provision
balance, leaving roughly $1.9bn of stated caution above pure base-case
modelling. Credit-risk normalisation is progressing, not finished.

## Limitations
- The documents do not disclose overlay composition by dollar amount (only
  qualitative drivers: seasoning, geography, releases in construction/
  mortgages/consumer finance), so the mix within the $238m cannot be verified.
- No segment-level scenario weightings are given — only a single Group figure.
- No pre-COVID or long-run average coverage ratio is disclosed, so whether
  125bps CAP/RWA is "high" or "low" versus history cannot be assessed from
  these documents.
- The $1.9bn base-case gap is a rounded chart annotation, not a reconciled
  note; the calculation method for the 100%-downside ECL scenario is not
  disclosed.
