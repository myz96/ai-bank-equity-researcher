# NAB FY25 Business & Private Banking: growth quality assessment

**Verdict: mostly value-accretive, broad-based growth — but not margin-neutral, and the latest NPL uptick is disclosed as single-name concentrated rather than systemic.**

## Analysis

Volume growth is real and broad-based. Business lending grew across "a broad range of sectors" (results book, p.24), and the presentation states "Broad based growth across most sectors including Trade, Manufacturing, Construction, Transport" (IP, p.50), with segment growth of Agri +3.5%, Health +5.9%, CRE +7.4%, Other +10.4% (IP, p.50) and SME market share gains. Deposits (+6.6%) kept pace with lending (+6.8% GLA growth), so expansion is largely self-funded, not wholesale-reliant.

Growth was not margin-neutral, though. NIM fell 8bps because of "competitive pressures impacting both the business and housing lending portfolios, as well as higher deposit costs including mix impacts due to term deposit growth" (results book, p.37). Revenue (net operating income +2.1%) grew far slower than lending volumes, and opex (+2.9%) outpaced revenue, producing negative jaws (CTI +30bps). Cash earnings on average assets fell 5bps to 1.25%, though cash earnings on average RWA held near-flat (2.18% vs 2.19%).

Credit signals are mixed but favor "broad-based, single-name-flagged" over "systemic deterioration." The FY25 impairment charge rose only 1.1% and its GLA ratio improved 1bp, and the increase "mainly reflects a higher charge for home lending, partially offset by a lower charge for business lending" (results book, p.37) — i.e., the modest total increase was not driven by business lending, where volumes actually grew fastest. Yet RWA grew faster than loans (+9.0% vs +7.3%) "due to growth in business lending volumes, combined with deterioration in asset quality" (results book, p.37), and business lending NPL/GLA rose to 3.22% from 2.66% a year earlier. Critically, the disclosure attributes the latest half's deterioration to concentration, not breadth: "Excluding two large well secured Agri customers, underlying NPL ratio down 3bps to 3.01%," with "Stable to improving NPL outcomes in 2H25 for most sectors" and a book that "remains well diversified and highly secured" (IP, p.27).

## Limitations
- No B&PB-standalone ROE is disclosed (only cash earnings/assets and /RWA).
- No dollar split of the impairment charge between housing and business lending — only qualitative direction is given.
- Sector growth (IP p.50) is on a "customer segment basis... not industry," so it does not map exactly to the ANZSIC-based NPL sector chart (IP p.27); exact reconciliation between the two is not possible from disclosure.
- FY24 comparatives were restated for a PB/B&PB portfolio transfer and an expense-presentation change, limiting like-for-like precision.
- The pre-2H25 four-half NPL uptrend (2.30%→3.22%) is only partly explained by the "two large Agri customers" callout, which addresses the most recent half only.
