# FY25 conversion of pre-provision/underlying profit into preferred earnings measure: CBA vs NAB vs Westpac

## Verdict
**CBA converted best.** It is the only one of the three where the pre-provision (top-line) measure *and* the preferred bottom-line measure both grew, and the bottom line grew faster than the top line. **Westpac converted second-best but from a declining base** — its bottom line fell less than its top line. **NAB converted worst**: its top line grew, but that growth was more than erased by the time it reached cash earnings, which fell.

## The numbers (source-reported growth rates, FY25 vs FY24)

| Bank | Top-line/PPOP measure | Growth | Preferred earnings measure | Growth | "Conversion spread" |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CBA | Operating performance (cash basis) $15,469m | +3% | Cash NPAT (continuing ops) $10,252m | +4% | **+~1pt (amplified)** |
| Westpac | Pre-provision profit ex-Notable Items $10.5bn | (3%) | Net profit ex-Notable Items $6,972m | (2%) | **+~1pt (cushioned)** |
| NAB | Underlying profit $10,965m | +1.3% | Cash earnings $7,091m | (0.2%) | **~(1.5)pt (reversed)** |

"Conversion spread" = the disclosed bottom-line growth rate minus the disclosed top-line growth rate; a positive spread means the swing factors below the pre-provision line helped, negative means they hurt.

## What explains the ordering
The main swing factor below the pre-provision line, for all three banks, was the **direction of the credit impairment charge** — tax and minority interests were minor for CBA/Westpac. CBA's loan impairment expense fell 9% ($76m), turning +3% pre-provision growth into +4% cash NPAT growth on a flat 30.5% effective tax rate. Westpac's impairment charge fell 21% (7bps to 5bps of average loans), cushioning a pre-provision *decline* into a smaller net-profit decline. NAB's impairment charge rose 14.4% to $833m, driven by individually assessed business-lending charges, which combined with a near-doubling minority-interest deduction to turn 1.3% underlying-profit growth into a 0.2% cash-earnings *decline*.

At the pre-provision line itself, CBA and NAB both grew because revenue-dollar growth outpaced expense-dollar growth despite mildly negative jaws in percentage terms (CBA income +5%/expenses +6%; NAB income +2.9%/expenses +4.6%). Westpac's pre-provision profit fell outright because expense growth (+9%, including a $273m restructuring charge) far outpaced revenue growth (+3%) — a cost problem starting above the impairment line, distinct from NAB's below-the-line credit problem.

## Comparability limits (these measures are not identical)
- **Different fiscal year-ends**: CBA to 30 June 2025; NAB and Westpac to 30 September 2025 — a three-month offset in the period measured.
- **Different "notable/non-cash" scopes**: CBA's cash-basis pre-provision figure still includes a $130m FY25 remediation/restructuring charge. NAB's underlying profit and cash earnings both retain its own $130m payroll remediation charge. Westpac's "Notable Items" cover only hedging items — its $273m restructuring charge is *not* excluded. A clean comparison would need to normalise for these differently drawn lines.
- **Continuing vs discontinued operations**: CBA and NAB disclose small discontinued-operations effects excluded from cash earnings; Westpac shows no such split.
- **Minority interests**: disclosed for all three, but material only for NAB.
- The filings do not reveal FTE-level cost detail or provisioning judgement quality behind the impairment swings; this note is limited to what each bank disclosed.
