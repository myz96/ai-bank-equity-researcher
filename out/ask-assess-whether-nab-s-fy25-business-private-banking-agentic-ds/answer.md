# Q: Assess whether NAB's FY25 Business & Private Banking growth was value-accretive rather than growth bought at the expense of margin or credit quality. Reconcile lending, revenue and cash-earnings growth with the NIM, impairment-charge and sector/NPL disclosures, and identify what is broad-based versus single-name driven.

*NAB, periods FY25 — confidence 82/100*

**Direct answer:** NAB's FY25 Business & Private Banking (B&PB) growth was only *partially* value-accretive: it was broad-based and the impairment charge was modest, but it was bought at the expense of margin (NIM down 8bps) and was accompanied by credit-quality deterioration, leaving returns per unit of RWA flat-to-down.

**Lending, revenue, earnings.** B&PB business lending rose 7.3% to $166.3bn and housing 5.8% to $109.3bn (GLAs +6.8% to $279.5bn), with non-housing growth "across a broad range of sectors" (ev-2, ev-24, ev-25). Net operating income rose 2.1% to $8,531m (NII +2.8% to $7,499m) and cash earnings +1.6% to $3,330m (ev-1, ev-8, ev-9).

**Margin.** NIM fell 8bps to 3.01% (from 3.09%), driven by "competitive pressures impacting both the business and housing lending portfolios, as well as higher deposit costs including mix impacts due to term deposit growth" (ev-3, ev-10). So growth was partly bought with margin. 2H25 NIM recovered 1bp to 3.02% (ev-3, ev-27).

**Credit quality.** The impairment charge was modest: CIC up only 1.1% to $529m, and CIC-to-GLAs actually fell 1bp to 0.19% (ev-6, ev-7, ev-12). But asset-quality ratios deteriorated: impaired assets-to-GLAs up 10bps to 0.49% and default-but-not-impaired up 31bps to 2.05% (ev-4, ev-5). Group NPLs rose 18.2% to $12,094m "mainly driven by deterioration in the Business and Private Banking business lending portfolio" (ev-18). 2H25 CIC jumped 23.2% HoH on "higher business lending charges driven by higher levels of impairment, asset quality deterioration and volume growth" (ev-14, ev-15).

**Broad-based vs single-name.** Growth was broad-based across sectors (ev-24). The credit deterioration was partly single-name: the default-but-not-impaired rise included "the downgrade of a small number of large customers in the Agriculture portfolio" (ev-19), and individually-assessed provisions rose 53.8% to $1,163m "primarily driven by... the business lending portfolio in Business and Private Banking" (ev-31, ev-32).

**Value-accretive test.** RWA rose 9.0% to $159.2bn on volume growth plus asset-quality deterioration (ev-11, ev-37). Cash earnings on average assets fell 5bps to 1.25% and on average RWA fell 1bp to 2.18% (ev-35, ev-36) — returns per unit of balance-sheet/RWA declined, so growth was not fully value-accretive. Combined B&PB+C&IB ROE was 18% (ev-28).

**What the evidence does not settle:** the note does not quantify the margin/credit trade-off in dollar terms, nor isolate how much of the NIM decline was growth-driven versus market-wide competition; the 18% ROE is a combined B&PB+C&IB figure, not B&PB alone.

## Limitations
- The note does not quantify the margin/credit trade-off in dollar terms, nor isolate how much of the NIM decline was growth-driven versus market-wide competition.
- The 18% ROE is a combined B&PB+C&IB figure, not B&PB alone, so it cannot be used to prove B&PB-specific value accretion.
- The Agriculture single-name downgrade is described qualitatively ('small number of large customers') without a dollar figure.
- B&PB figures are on a restated basis (portfolios transferred between Personal Banking and B&PB during FY25), so comparatives are not directly comparable to prior published numbers.
