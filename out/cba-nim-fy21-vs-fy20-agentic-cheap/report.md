# CBA — nim — FY21 vs FY20

**Movement (cash basis):** 207bps → 203bps (-4bps) | **Attribution confidence:** 92/100

*Read from: row 'Net interest margin (%) — Group Performance from continuing operations', column 30 Jun 20 -> column 30 Jun 21*

CBA's Group net interest margin declined 4 bps to 2.03% in FY21 from 2.07% in FY20 (cash basis). The decline was driven by higher liquid asset drag (-4 bps), lower asset pricing (-2 bps) from home loan switching and competition, and higher funding costs (-3 bps) from the falling cash rate environment. These were partially offset by favourable portfolio mix (+2 bps) from higher deposit funding ratios and TFF drawdowns, improved basis risk (+3 bps) from bill/OIS spread dynamics, and positive capital/other (-2 bps) from lower capital earnings, plus Treasury & Markets contribution (+2 bps). Ex-liquids, NIM was flat as pressure from lower rates was offset by mix and funding benefits.

### asset_pricing — "Asset pricing"
*-2 bps | confidence 90/100*

Decreased margin by 2 basis points driven by lower business lending margins (down 1 bps) from repricing actions to support businesses during COVID-19, and lower consumer finance margins (down 1 bps) from a reduction in the proportion of interest earning credit card balances. Home lending margins were flat, reflecting repricing (up 9 bps), offset by customers switching from variable to fixed rate loans (down 5 bps) and increased competition (down 4 bps) (ev-6).
> [ev-6] CBA/FY21/profit_announcement, printed p12: "Asset pricing: Decreased margin by 2 basis points driven by lower business lending margins (down 1 basis point) from repricing actions to support businesses during COVID-19, and lower consumer finance margins (down 1 basis point) from a reduction in the proportion of interest earning credit card balances. Home lending margins were flat, reflecting repricing (up 9 basis points), offset by the impact of customers switching from higher margin loans to lower margin loans, particularly from variable rate to fixed rate loans (down 5 basis points) and increased competition (down 4 basis points)."
