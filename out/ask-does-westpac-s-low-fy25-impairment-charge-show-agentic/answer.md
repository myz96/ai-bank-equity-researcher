# Q: Does Westpac's low FY25 impairment charge show that credit-risk normalisation is complete, or does the September 2025 provision stock still embed material caution? Reconcile the charge flow, the CAP/IAP movements, overlays and the probability-weighted/base/downside ECL scenarios.

*WBC, periods FY25 — confidence 88/100*

## Answer

**Both are true, but the second dominates.** The FY25 charge flow is genuinely normalised-to-benign — $424m, 5bps of average loans (FY24: 7bps), and only 4bps in 2H25. But the September 2025 provision stock still embeds material caution: reported probability-weighted ECL of $4,987m sits **$1.9bn above the 100% base case ECL of $3,040m**, the downside weight was lifted 5ppt over the year to 47.5%, and overlays were rebuilt +$59m to $238m. Normalisation is complete in the *flow*, not conceded in the *stock*.

### Charge flow
FY25 impairment charge $424m vs $537m FY24; 5bps vs 7bps (2H25 $174m, 4bps) [ev-1, ev-2, ev-26]. But the fall is not driven by better loss emergence: Westpac says it was "mainly due to an increase in write-backs and recoveries partly offset by a higher CAP charge" [ev-10]. Underlying loss realisation actually rose — CAP write-offs $561m vs $486m [ev-6]; net write-offs to average gross loans 6bps vs 5bps, i.e. *above* the 5bps charge [ev-3].

### CAP/IAP movements
- **IAPs** swung to a $34m net benefit from a $140m FY24 charge, on write-backs $195m (from $93m) and recoveries $247m (from $190m) [ev-4, ev-8, ev-9]. New IAPs were near-flat at $408m [ev-7]. The 2H25 IAP stock fall of $72m was a *single name write-off* in the trade sector, not credit improvement [ev-22].
- **CAP charge rose** to $458m from $397m [ev-5]. "Other changes in CAP" benefit shrank each half: (147)/(76)/(27) $m — "Improvement in economics, partly offset by overlays" [ev-39, ev-38].
- **Stock**: modelled CAP fell $168m to $4,201m; overlays +$59m to $238m; total CAP $4,439m; IAP $539m; total ECL $4,987m (‑2%) [ev-13, ev-14, ev-15, ev-16, ev-17].

### Overlays
Overlays rose $108m in 2H25 alone [ev-25], for "portfolio seasoning and geographical areas experiencing higher stress not included in modelled outcomes" [ev-23] — new caution, offsetting releases of construction/mortgage/consumer-finance overlays. Still below Sep-23's $432m [ev-33].

### Scenarios
Weights shifted *away* from benign: upside 5.0%→2.5%, base 52.5%→50.0%, downside 42.5%→47.5% [ev-18, ev-19, ev-20]. Downside assumes ‑6% GDP, 11% unemployment, ‑27% house prices, ‑32% commercial property [ev-27, ev-28, ev-30] — trough/peak values, not forecasts [ev-36].

### Counter-evidence (supports normalisation)
Mortgage 90+ 1.05%→0.70% [ev-12]; stressed exposures ‑17bps to 1.28% [ev-46]; Stage 2 CAP $2,390m→$2,087m [ev-35].

### Not settled
Coverage ratios fell — provisions/gross loans 63→58bps, CAP/credit RWA 130→125bps [ev-42, ev-43] — so caution is *reallocated*, not increased. Impaired exposures grew to $2,013m with cover down to 39.53% [ev-49, ev-50], and non-performing <90 days rose 7bps to 0.30% [ev-47]: early-stage formation is still building.

## Limitations
- The $1.9bn 'above base case' figure and the $3,040m/$7,152m scenario ECLs appear only in the investor discussion pack (slide, p54), not in the results announcement tables; no audited note reconciling them was located in scope.
- Westpac does not disclose a dollar decomposition of the CAP charge into the individual drivers it lists (commercial property/rates outlook, delinquencies, downside weight increase, new overlays), so the offsetting caution cannot be sized separately from the economic improvement.
- The overlay total of $238m is not broken down by overlay type; the released overlays (construction, Australian mortgages, consumer finance) and the new seasoning/geographic overlays are not individually quantified.
- The 100% base case and 100% downside ECL figures are point-in-time model outputs at a forecast date of 18 September 2025 and are not loss forecasts or guidance.
- No comparative 100% base case / 100% downside ECL figures for Sep-24 or Mar-25 were found, so the change in the absolute size of the $1.9bn buffer over the year cannot be established.
- The judgement that credit-risk normalisation is 'complete' in the flow is an inference from the printed bps and write-off figures; Westpac does not itself use the word 'normalisation' on the pages read.
- Whether the rise in non-performing <90 days past due (0.23% to 0.30%) reflects genuine early-stage formation or definitional/portfolio-mix effects is not explained on the pages read.
