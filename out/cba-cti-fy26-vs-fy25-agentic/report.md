## Limitations
- CBA publishes no waterfall of the cost-to-income RATIO itself. The ppt contributions in the driver table are my own arithmetic on the disclosed levels: income effect = 12,996/30,224 - 12,996/28,465 = -2.66ppt; expense effect = (13,755-12,996)/30,224 = +2.51ppt, of which the $40m increase in notable items accounts for +0.13ppt, leaving +2.38ppt from underlying expense growth. They sum to -0.15ppt against a printed (20)bpts move, the difference being rounding of the published ratio. Confidence is capped accordingly.
- The published expense walk (Inflation, Investment in technology, Investment in frontline and operations, Other, Productivity) is stated in $m on UNDERLYING expenses only, and its percentage annotations are contributions to underlying expense growth, not to the ratio. I have not converted individual bars into ppt ratio contributions.
- CBA qualifies the income driver: margins were 'broadly stable excluding growth in liquid assets and institutional reverse sale and repurchase agreements, which have broadly neutral impacts on net interest income' (ev-72) - so part of the 8% average interest earning asset growth carries little income, and reported NIM fell 3bpts to 2.05%.
- The productivity bar (-$404m) is a net offset inside the expense walk; CBA describes cumulative cost savings realised over the last 8 years of $2,472m (FY26) vs $2,068m (FY25) (ev-46) rather than an in-year gross saving, so the in-year gross saving is not separately disclosed.
- No statutory-basis cost-to-income ratio row is published; statutory income (+7%) and expenses (+6%) growth are shown as context only (ev-5, ev-6). FY25 comparatives in the FY26 book are restated for continuing operations, though the FY25 CTI level of 45.7% is unchanged from the FY25 book (ev-64).
