## Limitations
- Westpac publishes NO waterfall or bridge for the expense-to-income RATIO itself. The +4.46 ppt expense effect and -1.71 ppt income effect are my two-factor decomposition of the bank's own levels (11,916/21,763 = 54.75% as the intermediate step); they sum exactly to the +2.75 ppt delta, but the framing and the arithmetic are mine, not Westpac's, which caps their confidence.
- The primary FY24 -> FY25 walk I adopted (pack p27, sum check passed) is an EXPENSE walk in $m, not a ratio walk in ppt. Its six bars are reported in the expense driver narrative in dollars at the chart's own values; converting them to ppt would require choosing an income base the bank does not specify, so I have not done so.
- The ~1.2 ppt attributed to the $273m restructuring charge is my arithmetic (273/22,464). Westpac prints no expense-to-income ratio excluding the restructuring charge, only the 6% ex-restructuring expense growth rate (ev-41, ev-45).
- The Productivity bar is a gross -$402m offset inside a net +$972m expense increase (ev-24); Westpac describes productivity as providing only 'a partial offset' (ev-45) and lower occupancy costs as 'a partial offset' (ev-7). The walk's net expense effect therefore hides a substantial gross saving that was more than consumed by UNITE ($399m) and amortisation (+$106m) (ev-31, ev-32).
- There is no separate Group income walk for FY24 -> FY25 in either document, so the income driver rests on the growth rates and levels the bank states rather than on a bar of a chart.
- Westpac notes the ex-Notable measures are 'not defined by nor audited or reviewed' under Australian Accounting Standards; the headline ratio is a non-AAS measure.
