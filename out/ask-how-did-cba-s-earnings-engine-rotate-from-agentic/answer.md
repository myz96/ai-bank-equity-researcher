# Q: How did CBA's earnings engine rotate from FY25 to FY26, and was FY26's faster cash-NPAT growth higher quality despite reported NIM compression and a higher loan-loss rate? Reconcile the two years' income/expense/impairment mix with CBA's own FY26 margin bridge.

*CBA, periods FY26, FY25 — confidence 0/100*

## Answer

CBA's engine rotated from **margin-led to volume-led**. FY25's 4% cash-NPAT growth came from a +9bp NIM (2.08%) on almost no balance-sheet growth (AIEA +1%) plus a *falling* impairment charge (-$76m, loss rate 7bps). FY26's 7% cash-NPAT growth came from an +8% ($92bn) AIEA expansion that more than offset a 3bp NIM decline to 2.05%, with impairments *rising* (+$62m, loss rate 8bps). On CBA's own framing FY26 is the better-quality year — the 3bp NIM fall is entirely the neutral-margin liquids bar, underlying margin was "stable", jaws turned positive and asset quality improved — but two caveats bite: other-income growth included one-off gains, and 2H26 credit deteriorated sharply.

Jaws inverted: cost-to-income worsened 70bps to 45.7% in FY25 (ev-63) but improved 20bps to 45.5% in FY26 (ev-24); PACC +5% then +11% (ev-64, ev-25); ROE -10bps to 13.5% then +50bps to 14.0% (ev-59, ev-51).

**The FY26 margin bridge (2.08%→2.05%)** (ev-1): Liquids -3, Asset pricing -5 (home lending -4), Funding costs flat, Portfolio mix +2, Basis risk flat, Capital/Replicating/Other +5, Treasury & Markets -2. Excluding liquids, NIM was "stable" (ev-11). FY25's bridge was mix-reversed: Liquids & Pooled Facilities +7, Asset pricing 0, Funding costs -7, Portfolio mix -1, Capital/Replicating/Other +9, T&M +1 (ev-28) — underlying +2bps only (ev-57).

**Quality: mostly yes.** Growth was diversified (avg volumes: home +5%, business +10%, insto +11%, deposits +8%, ev-83); NPEs/TCE fell 4bps to 0.69% (ev-69); troublesome+NPE/TCE 0.97%→0.94% (ev-73); non-retail investment-grade +86bps (ev-45 set). Ex-notables opex grew 5.6% (12,866→13,585, ev-75), slower than 6.2% income.

**Against it:** other income leaned on one-offs — CommInsure milestone in commissions (+$135m, ev-81) and a Gemini IPO fair-value gain in other income (+$46m/+42%, ev-82); trading income fell 1% (ev-80). Credit worsened into 2H26: LIE $469m, +47% half-on-half, loss rate 9bps annualised (+3bps, ev-19, ev-47); net write-offs 6→7bps and NPE coverage 13.79%→13.40% (ev-68, ev-71); RBS LIE +39% to $378m (ev-44, ev-46).

## Limitations
- Neither document quantifies the CommInsure milestone payment or the Gemini fair-value gain individually, so the one-off contribution to FY26 other operating income cannot be sized.
- CBA publishes no decomposition of cash-NPAT growth into volume, margin, fee, cost and impairment contributions; the volume-vs-margin attribution here is inferred from the NII drivers and the NIM bridge it does print.
- FY25 comparatives in the FY26 book are restated ('Comparative information has been restated to conform with presentation in the current period'); e.g. RBS FY25 cash NPAT prints as $5,395m in the FY25 book and $5,330m in the FY26 book, and RBS FY25 LIE as $274m vs $272m. Divisional comparisons use the FY26 restated basis.
- Operating-expense growth commentary (6%) and the 5.6% slide figure are on an underlying basis excluding restructuring and notable items; statutory FY26 opex was $13,785m and cash $13,755m.
- The FY26 bridge bars were read off the chart image; the printed narrative on the same page confirms asset pricing (-5), portfolio mix (+2), Capital/Replicating/Other (+5) and Treasury & Markets (-2), but the -3bp liquids bar is only stated qualitatively in the text.
- FY24 loss-rate and pre-FY25 quality metrics were not read beyond the FY25 book's statement that the rate fell 2bps to 7bps; no FY24 profit announcement was in scope.
- 'Higher quality' is a judgement; it rests on CBA's own ratios (jaws, PACC, ROE, NPE/TCE) and is not a term the bank applies to the comparison.
