# Q: Is Westpac's targeted FY26 productivity enough to restore positive jaws, or is it mainly an offset to continuing wage, banker and UNITE investment pressure? Ground the judgment in the FY25 expense bridge, the investment-spend mix and the disclosed duration/expensing profile of UNITE.

*WBC, periods FY25 — confidence 84/100*

**Direct answer.** On the FY25 evidence, Westpac's targeted FY26 productivity (>$500m) reads mainly as an offset to continuing wage, banker and UNITE investment pressure, not as a route back to positive jaws. The FY25 bridge shows productivity (−$402m) recovering under a third of the +$1,374m gross cost build, and FY26 guidance re-arms every driver while UNITE's expensed spend keeps rising into FY29.

**FY25 expense bridge (FY24 $10,944m → FY25 $11,916m, +9%).** Staff costs +$397m (salary and EBA increases, superannuation, investment in bankers), technology +$146m, volume & other +$199m, investments +$359m (UNITE investment $399m; amortisation up $106m), restructuring charge +$273m — against productivity of only −$402m (sums of printed bars). Ex-restructuring expenses grew 6% while net operating income grew 3% (statutory): negative jaws of ~3ppt; on the ex-notable-items basis the cost-to-income ratio rose to 53.0% from 50.3%, and pre-provision profit fell 3% to $10,548m. 2H25 repeats the shape: productivity −$249m vs investments +$208m, staff +$143m, restructuring +$273m.

**Investment-spend mix.** FY25 spend was $1,918m (+9%): UNITE $660m (34% of spend, 74% expensed), growth & productivity $563m (30%), risk & regulatory $695m (36%). FY26 guidance: total ~$2bn with UNITE up to $850–950m at ~75% expensed — lifting expensed UNITE from ~$488m to ~$640–710m (my arithmetic) — partly offset by expected reductions in growth & productivity and risk & regulation spend and a moderating amortisation increase ($889m → $995m in FY25).

**UNITE duration/expensing.** The plan "extends into FY29"; UNITE is c.40% of total investment spend in FY27–FY28 with lower spend in FY29, and c.75% of spend is expensed. Benefits land only "after the initiative is complete", and disclosed completed-initiative savings are small (~$5m and ~$4m annual savings on $7m/$5m costs). Westpac frames UNITE as closing its cost-to-income gap to peers — a multi-year ambition, not FY26 jaws.

**Arithmetic.** >$500m of targeted productivity against EBA +3–4% on $6,326m staff costs, more bankers, ~$150–225m more expensed UNITE and still-rising amortisation leaves expense growth plausibly near FY25's 6% ex-restructuring pace; positive jaws would need revenue growth well above that, and no FY26 revenue guidance is disclosed.

**Not settled.** No explicit jaws target, no total UNITE benefit pool, no split of the >$500m between Fit for Growth and other productivity; every FY26 figure here is a target printed in FY25 documents — the corpus holds no FY26 WBC document.
