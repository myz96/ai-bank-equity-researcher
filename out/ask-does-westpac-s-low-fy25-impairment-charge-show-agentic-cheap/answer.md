# Q: Does Westpac's low FY25 impairment charge show that credit-risk normalisation is complete, or does the September 2025 provision stock still embed material caution? Reconcile the charge flow, the CAP/IAP movements, overlays and the probability-weighted/base/downside ECL scenarios.

*WBC, periods FY25 — confidence 82/100*

Westpac's low FY25 impairment charge does not signal that credit-risk normalisation is complete. The September 2025 provision stock still embeds material caution through a heavily tilted probability-weighting and rising overlays.

**Charge flow.** The total impairment charge fell to $424m (5 bps of average loans), down from $537m (7 bps) in FY24, with H2 at just $174m (4 bps). This was driven by higher write-backs (+$195m) and recoveries (+$247m) on individually assessed provisions, partially offset by a higher collective charge (CAP write-offs of $561m). Net write-offs to average gross loans were 6 bps.

**CAP/IAP movements.** Total ECL provisions declined 2% to $4,987m (from $5,096m a year earlier), driven by a $168m fall in modelled CAPs (to $4,201m) from improved commercial property and interest rate outlooks and lower mortgage delinquencies (1.05% → 0.70%). IAPs remained flat at $539m.

**Overlays.** Overlays rose to $238m (from $130m at March 2025 and $179m a year ago), up $108m in H2 alone. Westpac cites new overlays for portfolio seasoning and geographic stress not captured in modelled outcomes, partially offset by releases in construction, Australian mortgages, and consumer finance.

**Scenario weighting — the caution signal.** Despite improving fundamentals, Westpac increased its downside scenario weight by 5 percentage points over FY25 (to 47.5% at September 2025, from 42.5% a year prior), with a further 2.5ppt hike in H2. Upside weight fell to 2.5%. This means nearly half the probability-weighted ECL is driven by the downside scenario.

**Base vs downside gap.** Westpac discloses that provisions sit $1.9bn above the 100% base-case ECL ($536m forecast for 2025). The 100% downside ECL for 2025 is $1,223m — more than double the base case. The reported probability-weighted ECL of $4,987m thus reflects substantial downside risk embedded in the weighting.

**What the evidence does not settle:** The exact dollar contribution of the downside weight increase versus the overlay increase to the total provision level is not separately quantified. Nor are the specific probabilities applied to each scenario disclosed beyond the headline weights.
