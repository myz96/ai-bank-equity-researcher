# Q: How did CBA's earnings engine rotate from FY25 to FY26, and was FY26's faster cash-NPAT growth higher quality despite reported NIM compression and a higher loan-loss rate? Reconcile the two years' income/expense/impairment mix with CBA's own FY26 margin bridge.

*CBA, periods FY26, FY25 — confidence 88/100*

**Direct answer.** CBA's earnings engine rotated from margin-led to volume-led: FY25's NII growth (+5%) came from a 9bp NIM rise on just +1% average interest-earning assets, while FY26's NII growth (+7%) came from +8% AIEA ($92bn) against a 3bp NIM fall to 2.05%. FY26's faster cash NPAT growth (+7.1% vs +4.0%) was higher quality in mix terms — income-led with positive jaws — and the reported NIM compression is largely balance-sheet mix (liquids/repos) rather than core pricing, though credit costs ticked up (loan-loss rate 8bps, +1bp).

**Two-year mix (cash basis, continuing operations, $m).** FY25: NII 24,023 (+5%), other operating income 4,442 (+2%), total operating income 28,465 (+5%), opex 12,996 (+6%), LIE 726 (−9%), cash NPAT 10,252 (+4%). FY26: NII 25,586 (+7%), OOII 4,638 (+4%), TOI 30,224 (+6%), opex 13,755 (+6%), LIE 788 (+9%), cash NPAT 10,982 (+7%, +$730m). FY25 ran negative jaws (cost-to-income 45.0%→45.7%, +70bps); FY26 positive jaws (45.7%→45.5%, −20bps), with underlying opex +5.6% vs operating income +6.2% plus $170m restructuring/notables.

**FY26 margin bridge (2.08%→2.05%).** Liquids −3bps (liquid assets −3, institutional repos −1), asset pricing −5bps (home-loan competition −3, cash-rate lag −1, business/institutional −1), funding costs flat, portfolio mix +2bps, basis risk flat, capital/replicating & other +5bps (replicating portfolio +4, capital hedges +1), Treasury & Markets −2bps. CBA states NIM was stable excluding liquid-asset growth; the −5bps pricing drag was competition, partly offset by mix. Contrast FY25: +9bps reported, of which +7bps from lower liquids/pooled facilities — underlying only +2bps, with funding costs −7bps.

**Quality assessment.** Supporting higher quality: ROE +50bps to 14.0%, cash EPS +7% to 656.9c, DPS $5.05 (77% payout), deposit funding 79%. Offsetting: LIE +9% to $788m (2H26 +47% HoH off a low 1H), home-loan 90+ arrears 0.73% (+10bps HoH), and OOII flattered by one-off gains. But credit stocks improved: NPEs $11,113m (+1%), NPE/TCE 0.69% (−4bps), provisions/TCE 0.40% (−3bps), a $2.7bn scenario buffer, and the 8bps loss rate remains near historic lows (FY20: 33bps).

**Not settled.** FY27 margin trajectory (competition ongoing; investment spend held at ~$2.4bn); the bridge explains FY26 but does not quantify forward pricing; statutory NPAT (+8% to $10,911m) differs from cash.

## Limitations
- "Higher quality" is an analytical judgement, not a CBA-defined metric; it is assessed here on growth mix (income-led, positive jaws, stable underlying NIM) and credit-stock improvement.
- The dollar amount of FY26's one-off gains inside other operating income is not quantified on the pages read, so the underlying OOII growth rate cannot be computed.
- The FY26 margin bridge explains the reported year; it does not quantify FY27 pricing, and CBA gives no FY27 NIM guidance beyond holding investment spend at ~$2.4bn.
- All P&L figures are cash basis, continuing operations, unless stated; statutory NPAT (including discontinued operations) grew 7% to $10,866m and differs from cash NPAT.
- FY25 comparatives were restated to conform with FY26 presentation (footnote, FY26 profit announcement p2); FY25-as-reported figures cited here are from the FY25 document on its own basis.
- The 2H26 LIE increase of 47% is measured against an unusually low 1H26 ($319m); the full-year loss rate of 8bps remains near historic lows (FY20: 33bps).
