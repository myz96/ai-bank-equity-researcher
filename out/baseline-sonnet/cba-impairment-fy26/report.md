# CBA — Loan Impairment Expense: FY26 vs FY25

**Bank:** Commonwealth Bank of Australia
**Metric:** Loan impairment expense (LIE) — CBA's credit impairment charge
**Period:** FY26 (year ended 30 June 2026)
**Comparator:** FY25 (year ended 30 June 2025)
**Basis:** Cash basis, continuing operations. This line is identical between the cash and statutory income statements in both years — $788m (FY26) and $726m (FY25) both appear unchanged in the Consolidated (statutory) Income Statement, cross-referenced to the same Note 2.2 (CBA-FY26-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF p.88). There is therefore no cash/statutory divergence to reconcile for this metric.

## Movement

| | FY25 | FY26 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loan impairment expense | $726m | $788m | **+$62m (+9%)** |
| Loss rate (bps of average GLAA, annualised) | 7bps | 8bps | **+1bp** |

CBA's own FY25 profit announcement reported FY25 LIE as $726m at the time (CBA-FY25-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF p.16), so the comparator agrees exactly with the FY26 profit announcement's comparator column. (A small $2m segment-level reclassification between two divisions exists; see Disagreements.)

The $62m increase and 8bpts loss rate are confirmed consistently across all three FY26 documents:
- ASX Announcement: "$788m (Loan loss rate 8bpts) ... 9% on FY25" (PDF p.2)
- Profit Announcement: "$788 million, an increase of $62 million or 9% on the prior year" (PDF p.34)
- Results Presentation: "788", "+8.5%" vs FY25 (PDF p.24); loss-rate chart shows 7bps (FY25) to 8bps (FY26) (PDF p.29)

## Attribution

CBA's Note 2.2 provisions roll-forward (PDF p.118) reconciles the $62m movement by **provision type**, and the three lines sum exactly to the reported increase with **no residual**:

| Driver | FY25 | FY26 | Contribution | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Collective provisioning (net collective provision funding) | $456m | $606m | **+$150m** | 92 |
| New/increased individual (specific) provisioning | $439m | $422m | **-$17m** | 88 |
| Write-back of individually assessed provisions | $(169)m | $(240)m | **-$71m** | 88 |
| **Total** | $726m | $788m | **+$62m** | — |

150 − 17 − 71 = 62. This is an exact reconciliation from a single audited note, so it is treated as the primary quantified attribution.

### 1. Collective provisioning: +$150m (the entire driver of the increase)

Net collective provision funding rose from $456m to $606m. By segment:
- **Retail Banking Services**: LIE +$106m to $378m, "primarily driven by increased geopolitical risk, macroeconomic uncertainty, and rising cost-of-living pressures" (PDF p.34).
- **New Zealand**: LIE +$11m to $66m (NZD +$18m per Results Presentation, PDF p.56), "primarily driven by higher collective provisions reflecting deterioration in the unemployment outlook" (PDF p.34).
- **Business Banking** and **Institutional Banking and Markets** also cite higher collective provisions from portfolio growth and geopolitical/macroeconomic risk, but each segment's overall LIE fell because larger offsetting reductions in individual provisioning and write-backs outweighed this.

CBA does not split the $150m into volume (portfolio growth) vs asset-quality/overlay (risk migration, forward-looking scenarios) dollar sub-components.

### 2. New/increased individual provisioning: -$17m

Gross new/increased individually assessed provisions fell from $439m to $422m. Business Banking (LIE -$45m to $310m) and Institutional Banking and Markets (LIE -$16m to $33m) both name lower/released individually assessed provisions as a primary driver: "primarily driven by release of individually assessed provisions" (IB&M, PDF p.34). New Zealand names higher individual provisioning as a partial offset to its own collective-provision increase.

### 3. Write-back of individually assessed provisions: -$71m

Write-backs grew from $169m to $240m. Business Banking explicitly names "an increase in write-backs" (PDF p.34); separately, Corporate individually assessed provisions fell $6m, "driven by write-backs and write-offs" (PDF p.44).

### Cross-check: the segment lens (independent, also sums to $62m)

| Segment | FY25 | FY26 | Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retail Banking Services | $272m | $378m | +$106m |
| New Zealand | $55m | $66m | +$11m |
| Corporate Centre and Other | $(5)m | $1m | +$6m |
| Business Banking | $355m | $310m | -$45m |
| Institutional Banking and Markets | $49m | $33m | -$16m |
| **Total** | $726m | $788m | **+$62m** |

106 + 11 + 6 − 45 − 16 = 62. Two independent CBA disclosures (a provision-type note and a segment table) both reconcile exactly to $62m, which cross-validates the headline movement. The two lenses are not additive to one another — each fully explains the same $62m on its own terms.

### Unquantified drivers (named, not dollar-quantified)

- **Portfolio growth (volume).** Cited alongside macro/geopolitical risk as a driver of higher collective provisions (Business Banking, IB&M, Corporate collective provisions generally), consistent with GLAA growing 7% to $1,087.6bn at 30 June 2026. Confidence: 65.
- **Geopolitical risk, macroeconomic uncertainty, cost-of-living pressures and rising arrears.** Named in every segment's LIE commentary and the ASX Announcement: "Loan impairment expense increased mainly reflecting portfolio growth, cost-of-living pressures and increased geopolitical risk and macroeconomic uncertainty" (PDF p.2). Home loan and personal loan 90+ day arrears rose to 0.73% and 1.72% respectively. New Zealand separately cites "deterioration in the unemployment outlook." Confidence: 75.

## Residual

**$0.** The three quantified provision-type drivers sum exactly to the $62m movement (150 − 17 − 71 = 62), sourced from a single audited note (Note 2.2, PDF p.118).

## Disagreements

**FY25 segment split of loan impairment expense between Retail Banking Services and Business Banking.**
- CBA-FY26-profit-announcement.pdf (PDF p.34, FY25 restated comparative): RBS $272m / Business Banking $355m.
- CBA-FY25-profit-announcement.pdf (PDF p.34, FY25 as originally reported): RBS $274m / Business Banking $353m.

**Preferred:** the FY26 profit announcement's restated split, because it is the basis for this attribution and both documents footnote that "comparative information has been restated to conform to presentation in the current period." **Reason: restatement.** The $2m difference nets to zero at the Group level — both documents report the FY25 Group total as $726m.

The presentation's "8.5%" (PDF p.24) versus the profit announcement's "9%" (PDF p.34) is the same $62m/$726m figure at different rounding precision, not a genuine disagreement.

## Overall confidence: 90/100

The headline movement and loss rate are corroborated identically across all three FY26 documents and match the FY25 profit announcement's own figure. The primary driver decomposition sums to the movement with zero residual from a single audited note. Confidence is capped below the mid-90s because (a) the $150m collective-provisioning driver cannot be split into volume vs asset-quality/overlay dollars, and (b) a small, immaterial segment-level reclassification exists between the FY25 and FY26 profit announcements.

## Limitations

- "Net collective provision funding" ($150m) is a single group-level P&L line; CBA does not split it into volume / asset-quality / overlay dollars.
- Average GLAA (the loss-rate denominator) is not disclosed as an absolute $ figure; only period-end GLAA and CBA's own computed bps are given — the loss rate is quoted directly, not independently recomputed.
- New Zealand's LIE movement is in NZD in the Results Presentation (+NZD18m) versus AUD in the Profit Announcement (+A$11m); the AUD figure is used above.
- Half-year (2H26 vs 1H26) driver detail exists but was not used, since the comparator is FY25 (full year).
- Cash and statutory LIE were confirmed identical for both years via the Consolidated Income Statement (PDF p.88) cross-referenced to Note 2.2 (PDF p.118).
