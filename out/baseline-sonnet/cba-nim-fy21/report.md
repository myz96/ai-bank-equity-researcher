# CBA -- Net Interest Margin, FY21 vs FY20

**Basis:** Cash basis, continuing operations. **Comparator:** FY21 (year ended 30 June 2021) vs FY20 (year ended 30 June 2020).

## Movement

CBA's Group net interest margin (NIM) fell **4 basis points**, from **207bps (2.07%) in FY20 to 203bps (2.03%) in FY21**.

> "Net interest margin (%) 2. 03 [FY21] | 2. 07 [FY20] | (4)bpts" -- *CBA-FY21-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF page 22 (KPI table, "Group Performance from continuing operations")*

> "207 [FY20] ... 203 [FY21]" with the walk bars printed above summing to "(4)bpts" -- *CBA-FY21-results-presentation.pdf, PDF page 63 ("Group margin")*

This is the same figure the bank foregrounds in its FY21 highlights: *"Net interest margin (NIM) was down 4 basis points due to higher liquid assets, with the impact of the low-rate environment largely offset by management actions, lower wholesale funding costs and favourable funding mix."* (CBA-FY21-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF page 11).

The FY20 comparator (207bps) is unchanged across documents: the FY20 Profit Announcement independently reports 2.07% for FY20 (continuing operations, vs 2.09% in FY19), so the movement is not an artefact of the FY20-to-FY21 restatement noted in both Profit Announcements.

**Note on basis choice:** on an *including-discontinued-operations* basis, the KPI table shows FY20 at 2.08% and FY21 unchanged at 2.03%, i.e. a **5bps** fall. We prefer the continuing-operations, -4bps figure because it is the basis both documents use for the driver walk that follows (see Disagreements, below).

## Driver attribution

CBA discloses a full, reconciling seven-driver NIM walk for this movement in two places: prose in the Profit Announcement (PDF pages 32-33) and a waterfall chart in the Results Presentation (PDF page 63, "Group margin"). The two sources agree on every driver and the walk sums exactly to the -4bps movement, so the **residual is 0**.

### 1. Higher liquid assets: -4bps (confidence 96)

Non-lending interest earning assets (mostly liquids) grew \$20bn (12%) to \$195bn, funded by strong deposit inflows. These assets earn far less than loans -- 0.38% average yield in FY21 versus 3.25% on the total lending book -- so a bigger low-yielding pool dilutes the Group margin even though the dollar effect on net interest income was small.

- *"Liquid assets: Decreased margin by 4 basis points driven by increased lower yielding non-lending interest earning assets, including liquid assets."* (CBA-FY21-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF page 32)
- *"a \$17 billion increase in average liquid asset balances mainly due to strong customer deposit growth"* (CBA-FY21-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF page 32)
- *"Higher Liquids ... (4) ... Minimal impact on NII"* (CBA-FY21-results-presentation.pdf, PDF page 63)

### 2. Asset pricing: -2bps (confidence 96)

Business lending margin fell 1bp on COVID-19 support repricing; consumer finance margin fell 1bp as the interest-earning share of credit card balances declined. Home lending was net flat: repricing added +9bps, offset by customers switching from variable to fixed-rate loans (-5bps) and discounting under price competition (-4bps).

- *"Asset pricing: Decreased margin by 2 basis points driven by lower business lending margins (down 1 basis point) ... and lower consumer finance margins (down 1 basis point)"* (CBA-FY21-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF page 32)
- *"Home lending margins were flat, reflecting repricing (up 9 basis points), offset by ... switching from variable rate to fixed rate loans (down 5 basis points) and increased competition (down 4 basis points)."* (CBA-FY21-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF page 32)

### 3. Deposit pricing and funding costs: -3bps (confidence 95)

Earnings on transaction and savings deposits fell as the RBA cut the cash rate. The Profit Announcement nets this to -7bps; the presentation shows the same effect split into a cash-rate-cut impact of -8bps partly offset by +1bp of investment-deposit repricing. Both were partly offset by a higher replicating-portfolio benefit (+2bps) and lower wholesale funding costs (+2bps). This is consistent with the average deposit book: interest-bearing deposit cost fell from 1.30% to 0.61%, and the \$18.6bn Term Funding Facility drawdown cost only 0.23%.

- *"Funding costs: Decreased margin by 3 basis points, reflecting lower earnings on transaction and savings deposits mainly due to decreases in the cash rate (down 7 basis points), partly offset by higher benefits from the replicating portfolio (up 2 basis points) and lower wholesale funding costs (up 2 basis points)."* (CBA-FY21-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF page 32)
- *"Impact of cash rate cut (8) ... Investment +1 ... R. Portfolio +2 ... Wholesale +2"* (CBA-FY21-results-presentation.pdf, PDF page 63)
- *"Total interest bearing deposits 592,130 3,641 0. 61 [FY21] ... 561,221 7,304 1. 30 [FY20]"* (CBA-FY21-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF page 110)
- *"Term funding from central banks 18,646 43 0. 23"* (CBA-FY21-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF page 110)

### 4. Portfolio mix: +2bps (confidence 96)

The average deposit funding ratio rose from 71% to 77% as transaction and savings deposits grew strongly, customers switched to at-call deposits, and the TFF was drawn down (+4bps), partly offset by an unfavourable asset-mix effect (-2bps) from the decline in higher-margin consumer finance balances.

- *"Portfolio mix: Increased margin by 2 basis points driven by a higher average deposit funding ratio (30 June 2021: 77%; 30 June 2020: 71%) ... partly offset by an unfavourable impact from asset mix (down 2 basis points)"* (CBA-FY21-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF page 33)

### 5. Basis risk (incl. replicating portfolio): +3bps (confidence 94)

Basis risk is the spread between 3-month BBSW and the 3-month OIS rate. That average spread narrowed in FY21, lifting the margin, even as CBA says it structurally reduced its exposure through growth in cash-rate-linked deposits and the shift to fixed-rate home loans.

- *"The Bank's margin increased 3 basis points reflecting a decrease in the average spread notwithstanding a structural reduction in exposure to basis risk due to strong growth in cash rate linked deposits and a mix shift towards fixed rate home loans."* (CBA-FY21-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF page 33)

Confidence is slightly lower here (94) because neither document gives a sub-driver split for this bar, unlike most of the others.

### 6. Capital and other: -2bps (confidence 93)

Lower earnings on Group capital as rates fell cut margin by 3bps -- the equity hedge rolls onto lower rates over time -- partly offset by a higher New Zealand contribution (+1bp) from lower wholesale funding costs and favourable mix, itself partly offset by RBNZ cash rate cuts.

- *"Capital and other: Decreased margin by 2 basis points driven by lower earnings on Group capital due to the falling interest rate environment (down 3 basis points), partly offset by increased contribution from New Zealand (up 1 basis point)"* (CBA-FY21-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF page 33)

Confidence is slightly lower here (93) because the driver folds together two economically distinct effects (Group capital hedge earnings, and the New Zealand division) under one label, and we could not independently verify the "-3bps / +1bp" split against a separate data table.

### 7. Treasury and Markets: +2bps (confidence 95)

Higher bonds and commodities financing income in Global Markets lifted the margin by 2bps.

- *"Treasury and Markets: Increased margin by 2 basis points driven by higher bonds and commodities financing income in Global Markets."* (CBA-FY21-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF page 33)

## Disagreements

1. **Movement size depends on the reporting basis.** Continuing operations: -4bps (2.07% to 2.03%). Including discontinued operations: -5bps (2.08% to 2.03%) (both CBA-FY21-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF page 22). **We prefer -4bps, continuing operations**, because both the driver walk (PDF page 63) and its supporting narrative (PDF pages 32-33) are explicitly footnoted as being on a continuing-operations basis, and no driver walk exists for the including-discontinued-operations figure.

2. **Funding-cost driver granularity differs slightly.** The Profit Announcement nets the cash-rate effect to "down 7 basis points"; the presentation splits it into a cash-rate cut of -8bps and +1bp of investment-deposit repricing. **These are not in conflict** -- (-8) + 1 = -7, and the total -3bps for the driver is identical in both documents.

3. **A simplified 3-bucket view of the same movement also appears in the Results Presentation** (PDF page 29, "This Year"): higher liquids -4bps, impact of lower rates -6bps, management actions/other +6bps. This reconciles with the detailed 7-driver walk -- the six drivers other than liquids also net to zero (-2-3+2+3-2+2=0) -- so **we prefer the detailed 7-driver walk** (PDF page 63) as the primary attribution, since it carries full narrative support.

## Overall confidence: 95/100

The walk is disclosed identically, in whole basis points, in two independent CBA documents (the Profit Announcement's narrative text and the Results Presentation's waterfall chart), and it reconciles exactly to the stated -4bps movement with zero residual. Confidence is not higher because: the underlying calculation method is not disclosed, so the split cannot be independently recomputed from raw balance and rate data; some sub-driver figures were read from chart labels rather than a data table; and two drivers (basis risk, and capital and other) bundle multiple effects under one label without full independent corroboration of their internal split.

## Limitations

- All seven driver contributions are CBA's own internal margin-analysis decomposition. The documents do not disclose the underlying model, so the split cannot be independently recomputed from raw balance and rate data.
- The driver walk is presented on a cash, continuing-operations basis only. Neither source document publishes a statutory-basis NIM driver walk.
- Sub-driver figures inside each bar (e.g. home loan pricing +9bps, switching -5bps, discounting -4bps) were read from a bar chart (Results Presentation, PDF page 63) via its positioned text labels rather than a data table; they are precise to the nearest whole basis point as printed.
- The FY20 comparator (207bps, continuing operations) is identical between the FY20 Profit Announcement (as originally reported for FY20 vs FY19) and the FY21 Profit Announcement (as the restated FY21 comparator), so the restatement noted in both documents does not affect this movement.
- The quantified drivers sum exactly to the stated movement, so the residual is 0. This reflects that CBA's own walk is constructed to reconcile; it is not an independent check of the walk's accuracy.

## Format note (older-era disclosure)

This is an older reporting period. Unlike recent years, the FY21 Profit Announcement carries its NIM driver commentary as prose (PDF pages 32-33), not a labelled table or chart -- the only chart form of the walk is in the Results Presentation (PDF page 63). Both were read and cross-checked for this report.
