# CBA FY26 Cash Earnings Attribution

**Bank:** Commonwealth Bank of Australia (ASX: CBA)
**Period / comparator:** Full year ended 30 June 2026 (FY26) versus full year ended 30 June 2025 (FY25)
**Basis:** Cash net profit after tax (cash NPAT), continuing operations. FY25 comparatives are restated to FY26 presentation; the restatement does not change the FY25 group cash NPAT total.
**Sources:** FY26 Profit Announcement (PA26), FY26 Results Presentation (RP26), FY26 ASX Announcement (ASX26), FY25 Profit Announcement (PA25). All page references are PDF page numbers.
**Overall confidence: 96/100.**

## 1. The movement

Cash NPAT from continuing operations rose $730m, or 7%, from $10,252m in FY25 to $10,982m in FY26.

> "Cash net profit after tax ('cash NPAT' or 'cash profit') from continuing operations increased $730 million or 7% on the prior year to $10,982 million." (PA26, p26)

Cash NPAT including discontinued operations moved from $10,253m to $10,982m, up $729m or 7% (PA26, p16, p18). The $1m gap versus the continuing-operations figure is a discontinued-operations cash NPAT line that is nil in FY26 (PA26, p16).

The cash P&L bridge (PA26, p16, p27; all $m, FY26 vs FY25):

| Line | FY26 | FY25 | Impact on cash NPAT |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Net interest income | 25,586 | 24,023 | +1,563 |
| Other operating income | 4,638 | 4,442 | +196 |
| Underlying operating expenses | (13,585) | (12,866) | (719) |
| Restructuring and notable items | (170) | (130) | (40) |
| Loan impairment expense | (788) | (726) | (62) |
| Corporate tax expense | (4,699) | (4,491) | (208) |
| **Cash NPAT (continuing operations)** | **10,982** | **10,252** | **+730** |

The six impacts sum exactly to +$730m. **Residual: $0.**

## 2. Cash versus statutory NPAT

Statutory NPAT from continuing operations rose $778m, or 8%, from $10,133m to $10,911m (PA26, p18, p26). Statutory NPAT including discontinued operations rose $750m, or 7%, from $10,116m to $10,866m (PA26, p16, p18).

> "The Bank's statutory net profit after tax (NPAT) from continuing operations for the full year ended 30 June 2026 increased $778 million or 8% on the prior year to $10,911 million. The Bank's statutory NPAT (including discontinued operations) increased $750 million or 7% on the prior year to $10,866 million." (PA26, p26)

Non-cash items reconcile cash NPAT to statutory NPAT (PA26, p17; RP26, p23):

| Non-cash item (after tax, $m) | FY26 | FY25 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on acquisition, disposal, closure, demerger (continuing) | (17) | (172) |
| Hedging and IFRS volatility | (54) | 53 |
| **Continuing operations subtotal** | **(71)** | **(119)** |
| Discontinued operations (CFS disposal costs) | (45) | (18) |
| **Total non-cash items** | **(116)** | **(137)** |

Statutory NPAT (continuing operations) grew $48m faster than cash NPAT because the after-tax drag from non-cash items narrowed from -$119m (FY25) to -$71m (FY26). The FY25 drag was dominated by a $172m loss on disposals (Bank of Hangzhou and other divestments; a $156m post-tax loss on Bank of Hangzhou alone is separately disclosed at PA26, p136). The FY26 drag instead came from a -$54m swing in hedging and IFRS volatility. RP26 (p23) summarises this as "Statutory NPAT of $10.9 billion – broadly in line with Cash NPAT."

**Caution on the headline statutory figure:** ASX26 (p1) leads with statutory NPAT of $10,911m (up 8%) - the continuing-operations value, quoted alongside cash NPAT of $10,982m. The Group Performance Summary in PA26 (p16) also shows a total statutory NPAT of $10,866m (up 7%), after the $45m non-cash CFS-disposal loss in discontinued operations. Both are correct on their own basis; this report uses the continuing-operations pair for consistency with the cash NPAT comparison, since that is the basis on which the ASX announcement quotes them together.

## 3. Driver attribution

### Underlying drivers

**Driver 1 — Net interest income: +$1,563m. Confidence 98.**
NII rose 7% to $25,586m, the single largest driver of the increase, and volume did almost all of the work. Average interest earning assets rose $92bn (8%) to $1,246bn; average lending assets rose $60bn (7%), led by home loans (+$32bn, 5%) and business/corporate loans (+$27bn, 10%) (PA26, p28). Net interest margin fell 3bpts to 2.05%, though the bank states margin was stable excluding liquid-asset growth (PA26, p28). The bank's own NIM bridge (PA26, p28-29): asset pricing -5bpts (home lending -4bpts, business/institutional -1bpt, both from competition); funding costs flat; portfolio mix +2bpts; basis risk flat; capital, replicating and other +5bpts (replicating portfolio +4bpts, capital hedges +1bpt); Treasury and Markets -2bpts; liquids -2bpts. RP26 (p25) confirms the +$1,563m income-bridge block.

**Driver 2 — Other operating income: +$196m. Confidence 96.**
Other operating income rose 4% to $4,638m (PA26, p30). Commissions rose $135m (6%) to $2,234m, mainly on higher insurance income - including a milestone payment from the sale of Commonwealth Insurance Limited (CommInsure General Insurance) - higher equities income and higher syndication fees, partly offset by lower deposit, card and FX income. Other income rose $46m (42%) to $156m, mainly a fair value gain on the Gemini investment following its IPO, partly offset by lower Structured Asset Finance revenue after the aircraft lease portfolio sale. Lending fees +$12m, funds management +$12m, trading income -$9m. The one-off gains sit inside this driver; the bank names them but does not quantify them individually (PA26, p26, footnote 3).

**Driver 3 — Underlying operating expenses: -$719m. Confidence 98.**
Underlying operating expenses (excluding restructuring and notable items) rose 6% to $13,585m - the largest single drag on the movement. The bank's own bridge (PA26, p31; RP26, p27): inflation +$455m, investment in technology +$444m, investment in frontline and operations +$128m, other +$96m, productivity -$404m. By category: IT services +$393m (16%, cloud volumes, software licensing, amortisation, infrastructure/resilience/AI investment); staff expenses +$288m (4%, wage inflation, average FTE up 1,760 to 51,756); other expenses +$57m (4%, marketing); occupancy -$19m (2%, lower depreciation and favourable FX). A favourable FX effect is named as a partial offset to staff and occupancy costs but is not quantified.

**Driver 4 — Loan impairment expense: -$62m. Confidence 98.**
The expense rose 9% to $788m; the loan loss rate rose 1bpt to 8bpts of average GLAA (PA26, p34). Higher collective provisions reflect portfolio growth, geopolitical risk and macroeconomic uncertainty. Divisional moves (PA26, p34): Retail Banking Services +$106m (to $378m, cost-of-living pressures), New Zealand +$11m (to $66m), Corporate Centre +$6m, partly offset by Business Banking -$45m (to $310m, lower individually assessed charges and higher write-backs) and Institutional Banking and Markets -$16m (to $33m, release of individually assessed provisions). These parts sum to +$62m.

**Driver 5 — Corporate tax expense: -$208m. Confidence 97.**
Tax rose 5% to $4,699m on higher pre-tax profit (net profit before tax up 6%, from $14,743m to $15,681m). The effective tax rate fell 50bpts to 30.0%, mainly on lower non-deductible expenses (PA26, p35). Because tax grew more slowly (5%) than pre-tax profit (6%), the lower rate softened, but did not eliminate, the tax drag.

### Notable and restructuring items: -$40m incremental. Confidence 92.

These items are disclosed separately within operating expenses and rose from $130m (FY25) to $170m (FY26) - a $40m extra pre-tax drag versus the prior year (PA26, p27). The bank names but does not dollar-split the FY26 components: provisions for the settlement of legal proceedings in NZ; an additional goodwill payment to certain customers following ASIC's Better Banking review; and domestic customer remediation (PA26, p27, footnote 1). The NZ item is most likely the ASB Credit Contracts and Consumer Finance Act 2003 (CCCFA) class action, settled for NZ$135.6m and approved by the High Court of New Zealand on 14 January 2026 (PA26, p138) - though PA26 does not explicitly link the $170m figure to this settlement by name, so this connection is inferred, not quoted. FY25's $130m was fully split by the bank: domestic remediation $52m, NZ remediation $33m, Bankwest restructuring $45m (PA25, p27).

### Divisional view (cash NPAT, continuing operations; PA26, p16)

Business Banking +$452m (to $4,544m, +11%), Retail Banking Services +$257m (to $5,587m, +5%), Institutional Banking and Markets +$20m (to $1,258m, +2%), Corporate Centre and Other loss narrowed by $84m (to -$1,519m), New Zealand -$83m (to $1,112m, -7%).

## 4. Disagreements between sources

1. **Loan impairment expense growth rate.** PA26 (p16, p34) states 9%; RP26 (p24) states 8.5%. Both rest on the same dollar values ($726m to $788m, +$62m, which is +8.54%). This is a rounding difference (PA26 rounds to the nearest whole point; RP26 shows one decimal). **Preferred: use the dollar values.**
2. **Headline statutory NPAT.** ASX26 (p1) headlines the continuing-operations value ($10,911m, +8%); PA26 (p16) also shows the total value including discontinued operations ($10,866m, +7%). Both are correct on their own, fully disclosed basis. **Preferred: use the continuing-operations pair against cash NPAT**, since the bank quotes both headline figures together on that basis.
3. **FY25 divisional cash NPAT.** PA25 (p16) originally reported FY25: Retail Banking Services $5,395m, Business Banking $4,092m, Institutional Banking and Markets $1,224m, New Zealand $1,198m, Corporate Centre and Other -$1,657m. PA26 (p16) restates these to $5,330m, $4,092m, $1,238m, $1,195m, -$1,603m respectively. The group total ($10,252m) is unchanged in both. **Preferred: use the PA26 restated values** for any like-for-like divisional comparison, since PA26 states comparatives are "restated to conform with presentation in the current period." This disagreement has no effect on the group-level bridge in this report.

## 5. Limitations

- The bank does not split the $170m FY26 restructuring and notable items charge by its three named components, so the $40m year-on-year increase cannot be attributed to a single item with certainty.
- The CommInsure General Insurance milestone payment and the Gemini IPO fair value gain, both inside other operating income, are named but not individually quantified.
- The favourable FX offset inside operating expenses is described but not quantified.
- The bank discloses NIM drivers only in basis points; no dollar-value split of the $1,563m NII increase between volume and margin/rate effects is published.
- All figures are on the bank's cash, continuing-operations basis with FY25 restated comparatives. Expense and impairment driver signs are pre-tax P&L line impacts; the associated tax effect is captured separately in the corporate tax expense driver, not netted into each driver.
- Figures come from the text layer of the four named PDFs; chart-only figures (NIM bridge, expense bridge) were used only after confirming they matched the same numbers in text or tables elsewhere in the same documents.
