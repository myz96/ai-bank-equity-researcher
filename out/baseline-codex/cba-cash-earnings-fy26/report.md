# Commonwealth Bank of Australia: FY26 cash earnings attribution

## Conclusion

CBA's cash net profit after tax from continuing operations rose by **$730 million**, or **7%**, to **$10,982 million** in FY26 from a restated **$10,252 million** in FY25. CBA describes cash basis as management's view of operating results rather than cash accounting or cash flow (CBA-FY26-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF p.17, "The cash basis is used by management to present a clear view of the Bank's operating results."). The movement is directly disclosed (CBA-FY26-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF p.26, "increased $730 million or 7% ... to $10,982 million") and independently visible in the results presentation (CBA-FY26-results-presentation.pdf, PDF p.7, "10,252 10,982 ... +7.1%").

The central story was strong, volume-led revenue growth with broadly stable underlying margin, partly reinvested in technology and the franchise. Net interest income contributed **+$1,563 million** and other operating income **+$196 million** (CBA-FY26-results-presentation.pdf, PDF p.25, "Net interest income 1,563" and "Other operating income 196"). These were offset by **-$719 million** of underlying expense growth (CBA-FY26-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF p.31, "an increase of $719 million"), **-$40 million** of higher restructuring and notable items (CBA-FY26-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF p.27, "(170) (130)"), **-$62 million** of higher loan impairment expense (CBA-FY26-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF p.34, "an increase of $62 million") and **-$208 million** of higher corporate tax (CBA-FY26-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF p.35, "an increase of $208 million"). The disclosed operating-expense bridge shows that productivity provided a material **+$404 million** offset (CBA-FY26-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF p.31, "Productivity (404)"). The quantified bridge sums exactly to the cash-NPAT movement, leaving a **zero residual**.

## Basis and bridge convention

- Basis: cash NPAT from continuing operations for the full years ended 30 June 2026 and 30 June 2025.
- Comparator: FY25 comparatives as restated in the FY26 disclosure. CBA states that comparative information was restated to conform to current-period presentation (CBA-FY26-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF p.16, "Comparative information has been restated to conform with presentation in the current period.").
- Sign convention: positive contributions raise FY26 cash NPAT versus FY25; negative contributions reduce it.
- Measurement convention: revenue, operating-expense and impairment movements are disclosed cash-basis P&L deltas before tax. The full disclosed tax movement is shown separately. This is an algebraic cash-P&L bridge, not an invented allocation of after-tax effects to individual drivers.

## Source disagreements and treatment

1. **Continuing versus including discontinued operations.** FY25 cash NPAT was **$10,252 million** from continuing operations but **$10,253 million** including discontinued operations (CBA-FY25-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF p.16, "Net profit after tax from continuing operations ... 10,252"; CBA-FY25-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF p.16, "Net profit after tax ... 10,253"). I prefer $10,252 million because the FY26 operating commentary and every line in the attribution are continuing operations. Discontinued operations contributed $1 million in FY25 and nil in FY26 (CBA-FY26-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF p.27, "Net profit after tax from discontinued operations – cash basis – 1").

2. **Headline rounding.** The presentation reports FY26 cash NPAT as **$11.0 billion** and growth as **7.1%** (CBA-FY26-results-presentation.pdf, PDF p.5, "Cash NPAT $11.0bn"; CBA-FY26-results-presentation.pdf, PDF p.7, "10,252 10,982 ... +7.1%"). The profit announcement reports the exact **$10,982 million**, **$730 million** movement and headline **7%** growth (CBA-FY26-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF p.26, "increased $730 million or 7% ... to $10,982 million"). These are consistent after rounding. I use exact dollars and CBA's headline 7% description.

3. **FY25 divisional restatement.** The FY25 announcement showed Retail **$5,395 million**, Business **$4,092 million**, Institutional **$1,224 million**, New Zealand **$1,198 million** and Corporate Centre **$(1,657) million** (CBA-FY25-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF p.16, "Cash net profit after tax, by division"). The FY26 announcement restated those FY25 comparatives to **$5,330 million**, **$4,092 million**, **$1,238 million**, **$1,195 million** and **$(1,603) million**, respectively (CBA-FY26-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF p.16, "Comparative information has been restated"). Both sets sum to continuing cash NPAT of $10,252 million. The FY26 restated figures are preferable for current-period divisional comparisons; the group bridge is unaffected.

## Limitations

- CBA does not disclose dollar contributions for volume versus margin within the $1,563 million NII increase.
- CBA does not disclose dollar contributions for individual causes of the $196 million other-income increase.
- The underlying-expense waterfall categories are management bridge categories, not separately audited income-statement lines; the $96 million "Other" category is not decomposed.
- CBA does not publish after-tax contributions for each operating driver. Applying an assumed tax rate to each would be unsupported, so the analysis shows disclosed pre-tax P&L movements and the disclosed tax movement separately.
- Confidence scores are analyst probabilities that each claim would be judged correct against CBA's own disclosure, not figures reported by CBA.

## Overall confidence

**99/100.** The exact cash-NPAT movement and every quantified contribution reconcile directly to CBA's cash-basis P&L and management expense waterfall. The small remaining uncertainty lies in interpretation of management-defined expense buckets and unquantified subdrivers, not in the arithmetic.
