## Source disagreements
- **Cash vs statutory NPAT movement FY26 vs FY25** (definitional): +$730m / 7% cash (continuing) vs +$778m / 8% statutory (continuing) vs +$750m / 7% statutory (incl. discontinued)
  Preferred: +$730m / 7% cash (continuing). Cash NPAT excludes non-cash items that management does not consider representative of ongoing performance (ev-60). In FY26 those were a $62m loss on disposal/acquisition of controlled entities and $54m of hedging and IFRS volatility (ev-59), versus $190m and +$53m in FY25 (ev-61). Because the FY25 non-cash drag was larger, statutory profit grew faster than cash. These reconciliation items explain the same-period cash/statutory gap and are NOT year-on-year bridge drivers.
- **Loan impairment expense growth rate** (rounding): 9% (profit announcement) vs 8.5% (results presentation)
  Preferred: 9% (profit announcement). Both documents print $788m FY26 versus $726m FY25, a $62m increase (ev-41, ev-52). The profit announcement rounds the percentage to 9% while the presentation prints 8.5%. Same underlying dollars; the results-book figure ranks higher under the source hierarchy.
- **Operating expense growth rate: underlying vs headline** (definitional): +5.6% / +$719m underlying vs +5.8% / +$759m headline (13,755 vs 12,996)
  Preferred: +$719m underlying, with notables shown separately. The bank presents opex on both an underlying and headline basis (ev-27-source). Underlying opex of $13,585m vs $12,866m is +$719m/5.6% (ev-2, ev-33, ev-51); total opex of $13,755m vs $12,996m is +5.8% (ev-54-presentation p54 KPI). The method requires the underlying number as the expense driver with the $40m notables delta claimed separately, which together reproduce the headline movement.

## Limitations
- The bank publishes no single cash-NPAT waterfall for FY26 vs FY25; my bridge is assembled from the Group Performance Summary table's component rows (ev-1 to ev-7) plus the bank's stated component movements. The components sum exactly to the $730m movement, so the residual is zero, but the framing is mine, not a published walk.
- The two primary walk charts I adopted are sub-bridges, not the earnings bridge: the results book's underlying operating expense walk (ev-32, sum check passed) decomposes only the $719m expense bar, and the presentation's operating income walk (ev-54, sum check passed) decomposes only the $1,759m income increase into NII and other income.
- The NII movement is not split into dollar volume and dollar margin effects by the bank. The nii.volume/nii.margin split is therefore left unquantified in dollars; only the bps NIM walk components are available (asset pricing -5bpts, portfolio mix +2bpts, funding costs and basis risk flat).
- The notable-items delta of -$40m is my own subtraction of the two annual columns; the bank prints only the levels ($170m, $130m) and a 31% change, so this contribution is capped below a bank-stated movement.
- QUALIFICATION THAT CHANGES MEANING: the reported 3bpt NIM decline is materially a liquids artefact — the bank states that excluding growth in liquid assets, which have broadly neutral impacts on net interest income, NIM was stable (ev-24). Reading the 3bpt fall as an earnings drag would overstate its effect.
- Within the expense walk, the +$455m inflation, +$444m technology and +$128m frontline gross increases are partly funded by -$404m of productivity benefits (ev-32); the $719m net bar hides that gross/offset structure.
- Cash NPAT including discontinued operations is also $10,982m in FY26 versus $10,253m in FY25 (ev-61), a $729m movement — $1m different from the continuing-operations $730m. I built the bridge on the continuing-operations basis the bank uses throughout its performance analysis.
- I did not read the segment-level income and expense tables, the ASX announcement, or the transcript, so division-level attribution of the income and expense bars beyond what the group narrative pages state is not verified.
- Research stopped early: the tool-call budget (40 calls) was reached, so the evidence behind this answer is less complete than a full run's.
- Expenses are claimed on the underlying/notable split; the bank equally publishes the combined headline framing, so both claims are capped at 80.
