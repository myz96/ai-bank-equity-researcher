## Limitations
- CBA does not publish a single cash-NPAT waterfall for FY26 vs FY25. I built the bridge from the results book's Group Performance Summary component columns (ev-1 to ev-8), corroborated by two PRIMARY walks (operating income FY25->FY26 and underlying operating expenses FY25->FY26) whose sum checks passed. The components reconcile exactly: +1,563 NII +196 OOI -719 underlying opex -40 notables -62 LIE -208 tax = +730, so residual is zero. The additivity framing is mine, not a published CBA bridge.
- The tax contribution of -$208m is my own subtraction of the FY26 and FY25 corporate tax expense columns, not a movement CBA states in dollars; confidence capped at 78 accordingly. I did not read the tax note (page 35) that would explain the effective tax rate fall from 30.5% to 30.0%, so the reason behind the tax movement rests only on the rate figure printed in the presentation summary rather than on CBA's own tax commentary.
- NII is NOT split into volume and margin dollars by CBA. The margin driver is quantified only in bps (-3bpts, walk ev-66) and the volume driver only in balance-sheet dollars ($92bn AIEA, $60bn lending). Neither can be converted to a $m earnings contribution from the evidence, so nii.volume carries no contribution and nii.margin carries a bps unit that must not be added into the $m bridge — nii at +$1,563m is the additive component.
- QUALIFICATION CBA ITSELF MAKES that changes what the NIM fall means: the largest single negative in the margin walk, liquids and repos (-3bpts in the results book walk, -4bpts in the slide walk), is explicitly labelled "Minimal impact on earnings" and "broadly neutral impact on net interest income", because the $15bn liquid-asset and $11bn repo growth carries matching funding. Excluding liquids, CBA says NIM was STABLE. So the headline 3bpt NIM decline overstates the earnings drag, and the true margin story is competition (-5bpts asset pricing) offset by hedge earnings (+5bpts capital/replicating).
- The two NIM walks differ in framing: the results book prints seven bars (Liquids -3, Asset pricing -5, Funding costs 0, Portfolio mix +2, Basis risk 0, Capital/Replicating and Other +5, Treasury & Markets -2) while the slide prints six (Liquids & repos -4, Asset pricing -5, Funding costs 0, Portfolio mix +2, Interest rate risk hedging +5, Treasury & Markets -1). I adopted the results book walk per source hierarchy and did not mix bars across the two.
- The expense walk's "Other" bar of +$96m is not explained by any narrative I read, and the composition of the +$455m inflation and +$444m technology bars against the income-statement category movements (staff +$288m, IT services +$393m, occupancy -$19m, other +$57m) is a different cut that CBA does not reconcile to the walk.
- Corroboration is adequate but uneven: the movement, operating income, pre-provision profit and cash/statutory NPAT are confirmed across the profit announcement, the presentation and the ASX announcement, and FY25 comparatives were checked against the FY25 profit announcement. The notable-items delta (-$40m) and the tax delta (-$208m) appear in only the profit announcement columns, so those two sit at or below 85.
- I did not read the FY26 divisional pages in full, so the attribution is Group-level. Divisional cash NPAT levels are cited (RBS $5,587m vs $5,330m, Business Banking $4,544m vs $4,092m +11%) but I have not verified the drivers behind each division's contribution.
- Research stopped early: the tool-call budget (40 calls) was reached, so the evidence behind this answer is less complete than a full run's.
