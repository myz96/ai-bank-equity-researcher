# Q: Across CBA, NAB and Westpac in FY25, which bank best converted its disclosed underlying top-line or pre-provision performance into its preferred cash/core earnings measure, and what explains the ordering? Compare direction and drivers rather than absolute dollars, and state the comparability limits of the three non-identical profit measures.

*CBA, NAB, WBC, periods FY25 — confidence 85/100*

## Answer

**CBA converted best.** It is the only one of the three whose top line, its pre-provision line and its preferred earnings measure all rose, and the only one whose preferred measure grew faster than the pre-provision line: pre-provision profit +3%, cash NPAT +4% (FY25 v FY24, cash basis) [ev-26, ev-38]. Westpac is second on *conversion* though negative on both legs (pre-provision profit −3%, net profit ex Notable Items −2%) [ev-8, ev-37]; **NAB converted worst** — the only bank to turn a *positive* pre-provision result into a *negative* earnings result (underlying profit +1.3%, cash earnings −0.2%) [ev-19, ev-20].

## The ordering, and what drives it

The gap between the two lines is set almost entirely by the impairment charge:

| | Top line | Pre-provision | Impairment | Preferred measure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CBA (cash) | income +5% [ev-38] | +3% [ev-26] | LIE −$76m to $726m, loss rate 7bpts [ev-38, ev-24] | cash NPAT +4% [ev-27] |
| WBC (ex Notable Items) | revenue +3% [ev-36] | −3% [ev-37] | −$537m→−$424m, −21%; 7→5bps [ev-8, ev-12] | −2% to $6,972m [ev-9] |
| NAB (cash) | income +2.9% [ev-1] | +1.3% [ev-19] | +$105m/+14.4% to $833m [ev-44] | cash earnings −$11m/−0.2% [ev-43] |

CBA and Westpac both had a **falling** charge that added roughly a point of growth back; NAB's rose 14.4% on individually assessed charges "primarily in Corporate and Institutional Banking" [ev-44], plus tax +$27m [ev-45] — enough to consume its underlying gain.

Costs explain the pre-provision leg. CBA: income +5% vs underlying expenses +6% [ev-38] — jaws slightly negative (underlying cost-to-income 44.6%→45.2%) [ev-48]. NAB: +2.9% income vs +4.6% expenses, incl. $130m payroll review/remediation [ev-46]. Westpac is worst: +3% revenue vs **+9%** expenses (+6% excluding a $273m Fit for Growth restructuring charge) [ev-11], the reason its pre-provision line fell despite revenue growth [ev-10]. Its own bridge attributes −$972m to expenses against +$557m NII and +$144m non-interest income, with +$113m from impairments [ev-29].

## Comparability limits

The three measures are not one measure. CBA's $15,469m "pre-provision profit" is headline cash and still **includes** $130m of restructuring/notable items [ev-47]; Westpac's $10,548m is explicitly *excluding Notable Items* [ev-6]; NAB's $10,965m "underlying profit" is cash-basis with no notables adjustment [ev-19]. Both WBC's and NAB's measures are non-AAS, unaudited [ev-13, and NAB's cash earnings note]. Periods differ: CBA is 30 June FY, NAB/WBC 30 September. NAB restated FY24 comparatives for an expense-presentation change [ev-19].

## Limitations
- The three 'pre-provision' measures are not defined identically: CBA's $15,469m operating performance/pre-provision profit still includes $130m of restructuring and notable items (ev-47), whereas Westpac's $10,548m is stated excluding Notable Items (ev-6) and NAB's $10,965m underlying profit carries no notables adjustment (ev-19). Any 'conversion' ranking is therefore approximate.
- Reporting periods are not aligned: CBA's FY25 ends 30 June 2025; NAB's and Westpac's end 30 September 2025. The banks faced different quarters of rate and credit conditions.
- The question asks about conversion of top-line/pre-provision into earnings. None of the three banks publishes a 'conversion ratio'; the ordering here is my arithmetic comparison of the banks' own printed growth percentages, not a disclosed metric.
- CBA's cash NPAT growth is stated on a continuing-operations basis with FY24 comparatives restated (ev-17); NAB restated FY24 comparatives for an expense-presentation change and for portfolio transfers between divisions (ev-19). Growth rates therefore sit on restated bases.
- I did not read a CBA cash-NPAT movement waterfall equivalent to Westpac's ev-29 bridge, so CBA's driver decomposition is taken from narrative percentages rather than dollar bars. I did not locate a comparable NAB FY24-FY25 dollar bridge either.
- Westpac's preferred measure is 'net profit excluding Notable Items', not a 'cash' measure; CBA and NAB use cash NPAT/cash earnings. The measures are not interchangeable and neither WBC's nor NAB's is audited (ev-13, ev-49).
- ROE is quoted for CBA (13.5%, ev-25) and NAB (11.4%, ev-21) but I did not read Westpac's ROE, so the three-way returns comparison is incomplete.
