# CBA — impairment — FY26 vs FY25

**Movement (cash basis):** 726$m → 788$m (+62$m) | **Attribution confidence:** 86/100

*Read from: row 'Total loan impairment expense', column Full Year Ended 30 Jun 25 $M -> column Full Year Ended 30 Jun 26 $M*

CBA's loan impairment expense rose to $788m in FY26 from $726m in FY25, an increase of $62m or 9% (the slide pack prints the same movement as 8.5%). The loss rate rose 1bp to 8bps of average gross loans and acceptances, with corporate improving 14→12bps and consumer 5→6bps. The whole increase is a collective-provision story: net collective provision funding rose $150m to $606m on portfolio growth, increased geopolitical risk and macroeconomic uncertainty, with the Downside scenario weighting increased during the year. Individually assessed provisioning went the other way — new and increased IAP fell $17m to $422m and write-backs rose $71m to $240m, an $88m offset. By division, Retail +$106m to $378m and New Zealand +$11m to $66m drove the rise, partly offset by Business Banking -$45m to $310m and Institutional -$16m to $33m. Total provision stock rose only $99m to $6,476m.

## Limitations
- CBA publishes no waterfall/bridge chart for the FY26 vs FY25 impairment charge. The quantified bridge is built by subtracting the two full-year columns of the provision-type rows in note 2.2 (p118), so the arithmetic is mine, not the bank's framing — hence confidence capped below chart level. The three components (+150, -17, -71) sum exactly to the stated +$62m, leaving zero residual.
- The bank does not quantify how much of the $150m collective bar is portfolio growth versus risk migration versus FLA/MES changes; collective.volume and overlays_fla are therefore carried unquantified rather than assigned borrowed numbers.
- Divisional deltas are the 'where', not a second bridge — they must not be added to the provision-type bridge. Note the FY26 divisional table's Corporate Centre and Other moved from a $5m benefit to a $1m expense.
- Provision-stock movements quoted in the narratives (corporate collective +$172m to $2,797m, consumer collective -$48m to $2,888m, total provisions +$99m to $6,476m) are balance-sheet levels that also absorb write-offs and recoveries; they are context and are not the period's charge.
- The consumer collective provision DECREASE of $48m sits beside a Retail charge INCREASE of $106m — the stock fell because write-offs ($618m) exceeded funding, so the stock direction must not be read as the charge direction.
- Sectoral FLA commentary on presentation p71 is explicitly framed as 'last 6 months', a half-year span, so it annotates rather than explains the full-year movement.
