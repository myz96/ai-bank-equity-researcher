# CBA FY21 Net Interest Margin: Movement and Attribution

**Coverage:** Commonwealth Bank of Australia, year ended 30 June 2021 vs year ended 30 June 2020.
**Basis:** Cash basis, continuing operations. All driver figures are CBA's own disclosure.
**Sources:** FY21 Profit Announcement (PA21), FY21 Results Presentation (RP21), FY20 Profit Announcement (PA20). PDF page numbers refer to the physical page in each file.

---

## 1. The movement

Group NIM fell 4 basis points, from 2.07% in FY20 to 2.03% in FY21.

- PA21, PDF p22: "Net interest margin (%) 2.03 [vs] 2.07 (4)bpts" (continuing operations KPI table).
- PA21, PDF p32: "The Bank's net interest margin decreased 4 basis points on the prior year to 2.03%."
- RP21, PDF p57: NIM bars "207 [FY20] 203 [FY21] (4)bpts".
- PA20, PDF p22, confirms the comparator as originally reported: "Net interest margin (%) 2.07". The FY21 restatement of comparatives did not change this value.

On an including-discontinued-operations basis the fall is 5 bps (2.08% to 2.03%; PA21, PDF p22). I use the continuing-operations basis because CBA presents the driver walk on that basis (RP21, PDF p63, footnote: "Presented on a continuing operations basis").

Half-year path: 1H21 2.01%, 2H21 2.04% (PA21, PDF p11: "2.04% 2H21, 1H21 2.01% +3bpts"). The margin fell early in the year and partly recovered in the second half.

CBA's one-line summary (PA21, PDF p9): "Group NIM declined due to higher liquid assets and the ongoing impact of a low interest rate environment, partly offset by management actions, lower wholesale funding costs and favourable funding mix."

## 2. Driver attribution (FY20 2.07% -> FY21 2.03%)

The PA21 narrative (PDF pp32-33) and the RP21 waterfall (PDF p63) give the same seven-driver walk. The drivers sum exactly to -4 bps. **Residual: 0 bps.**

| # | Driver | bps | Confidence |
|---|--------|-----|------------|
| 1 | Higher liquid assets | -4 | 97 |
| 2 | Asset pricing | -2 | 97 |
| 3 | Funding costs (deposit pricing & funding) | -3 | 96 |
| 4 | Portfolio mix | +2 | 97 |
| 5 | Basis risk (incl. replicating portfolio) | +3 | 96 |
| 6 | Capital and other | -2 | 96 |
| 7 | Treasury and Markets | +2 | 96 |
| | **Total** | **-4** | **95 overall** |

### Driver 1: Higher liquid assets, -4 bps (confidence 97)

Deposit inflows lifted low-yield liquid assets, which diluted the margin.

- PA21, PDF p32: "Liquid assets: Decreased margin by 4 basis points driven by increased lower yielding non-lending interest earning assets, including liquid assets."
- PA21, PDF p32: non-lending interest earning assets rose $20bn (12%) to $195bn, with "a $17 billion increase in average liquid asset balances mainly due to strong customer deposit growth".
- RP21, PDF p63, marks this bar "Minimal impact on NII". This is a mix/denominator effect. The income loss is small; the ratio falls because the asset base grows faster than income.
- Sensitivity for context (RP21, PDF p64): "Every additional $10bn of liquid assets is expected to reduce Group NIM by ~2bpts."

### Driver 2: Asset pricing, -2 bps (confidence 97)

- Business lending: -1 bp "from repricing actions to support businesses during COVID-19" (PA21, PDF p32).
- Consumer finance: -1 bp "from a reduction in the proportion of interest earning credit card balances" (PA21, PDF p32).
- Home lending: net flat. "Repricing (up 9 basis points), offset by the impact of customers switching from higher margin loans to lower margin loans, particularly from variable rate to fixed rate loans (down 5 basis points) and increased competition (down 4 basis points)" (PA21, PDF p32). RP21, PDF p63, shows the same split: Pricing +9, Switching (5), Discounting (4).

### Driver 3: Funding costs, -3 bps (confidence 96)

Lower rates cut the value of transaction and savings deposits. Cheaper wholesale funding and the replicating portfolio gave partial offset.

- PA21, PDF p32: "Funding costs: Decreased margin by 3 basis points, reflecting lower earnings on transaction and savings deposits mainly due to decreases in the cash rate (down 7 basis points), partly offset by higher benefits from the replicating portfolio (up 2 basis points) and lower wholesale funding costs (up 2 basis points)."
- RP21, PDF p63, splits the deposit effect further: "Impact of cash rate cut (8), Investment +1, R. Portfolio +2, Wholesale +2". Both versions sum to -3 bps. See Section 4.
- Rate tables support the wholesale and TFF benefit (PA21, PDF p110): average interest-bearing deposit cost fell from 1.30% to 0.61%; debt issues cost fell from 1.65% to 0.84%; "Term funding from central banks 18,646 [avg bal, $M] ... 0.23 [%]".

### Driver 4: Portfolio mix, +2 bps (confidence 97)

- PA21, PDF p33: "+2 basis points driven by a higher average deposit funding ratio (30 June 2021: 77%; 30 June 2020: 71%) due to strong growth in transaction and savings deposits, customers switching to at-call deposits, and the drawdown of the TFF (up 4 basis points), partly offset by an unfavourable impact from asset mix (down 2 basis points), mainly due to a decline in higher margin consumer finance balances."
- RP21, PDF p63, shows the same +4 / (2) split.

### Driver 5: Basis risk (incl. replicating portfolio), +3 bps (confidence 96)

- PA21, PDF p33: "The Bank's margin increased 3 basis points reflecting a decrease in the average spread notwithstanding a structural reduction in exposure to basis risk due to strong growth in cash rate linked deposits and a mix shift towards fixed rate home loans." Basis risk here is the 3-month BBSW vs 3-month OIS spread.
- RP21, PDF p64, notes the sensitivity: "As at Jun 21, every 14 bpts = ~1bpt of Group NIM."

### Driver 6: Capital and other, -2 bps (confidence 96)

- PA21, PDF p33: "-2 basis points driven by lower earnings on Group capital due to the falling interest rate environment (down 3 basis points), partly offset by increased contribution from New Zealand (up 1 basis point)."
- Cross-check (PA21, PDF p108): the benefit of free funds fell from 0.21% to 0.13% (-8 bps) while the interest spread rose from 1.86% to 1.90% (+4 bps). This confirms that lower rates cut the value of equity and interest-free funding.
- Hedge context (RP21, PDF p64): the deposit hedge and equity hedge tractor rates keep falling; the deposit hedge 2H21 average tractor was 1.22% with a 1.18% exit rate.

### Driver 7: Treasury and Markets, +2 bps (confidence 96)

- PA21, PDF p33: "Treasury and Markets: Increased margin by 2 basis points driven by higher bonds and commodities financing income in Global Markets."

## 3. Reconciliation and residual

-4 (liquids) - 2 (asset pricing) - 3 (funding costs) - 2 (capital and other) + 2 (portfolio mix) + 3 (basis risk) + 2 (Treasury & Markets) = **-4 bps. Residual: 0.**

Each driver's sub-components also sum to the driver total (asset pricing: -1 -1 +0 = -2; funding costs: -7 +2 +2 = -3; portfolio mix: +4 -2 = +2; capital and other: -3 +1 = -2).

## 4. Disagreements between sources

1. **Basis of the movement.** -4 bps on continuing operations vs -5 bps including discontinued operations (both PA21, PDF p22). I prefer -4 bps because the driver walk uses the continuing-operations basis.
2. **Funding costs sub-split.** PA21 (PDF p32) nets the deposit effect to -7 bps. RP21 (PDF p63) shows -8 bps from the cash rate cut plus +1 bp from investment deposit repricing. Both sum to -3 bps. This is a presentation difference, not a conflict. I prefer the presentation's finer split for analysis and the PA for the total.
3. **Alternative aggregation.** RP21, PDF p29, re-groups the same movement as: higher liquids -4, impact of lower rates -6, management actions/other +6. This view is consistent with the seven-driver walk and confirms the shape of the story: rates and liquids hurt; management actions (repricing, funding mix, TFF) offset most of the rate pressure.

## 5. Supporting context (not additive to the Group walk)

- Divisional NIM (PA21, PDF p24): RBS 2.60% (-3 bps); Business Banking 2.98% (-12 bps); Institutional Banking and Markets 1.00% (+2 bps); ASB New Zealand 2.18% (+6 bps, NZD).
- Net interest income rose 1% to $18,839m: average interest earning assets +$32bn (4%) to $930bn, offset by the -4 bps margin (PA21, PDF p32).
- FY22 guidance (PA21, PDF p11): headwinds from "the continuing low-rate environment, price competition ..., customers switching to fixed rate home loans and higher rate deposits, and higher liquids", partly offset by TFF funding benefits. RP21, PDF p64: "In FY22 the hedge earnings decline is expected to be neutralised by the benefit of lower funding costs due to the Term Funding Facility."

## 6. Confidence and limitations

**Overall confidence: 95/100.** The movement and every driver value come verbatim from CBA's own disclosure, and two documents agree on every figure.

Limitations:

- The driver decomposition is CBA's internal calculation. The documents do not disclose the method, so I cannot recompute the split from raw data.
- Figures are rounded to whole basis points.
- FY20 comparatives were restated in PA21 (Note 1.1 of the 2021 Annual Report); the restated FY20 NIM equals the originally reported 2.07%, so the comparator is unaffected.
- No statutory-basis NIM walk exists; the analysis is cash basis, continuing operations.
