# CBA FY26 loan impairment expense: movement attribution

**Coverage:** Commonwealth Bank of Australia (CBA), full year ended 30 June 2026 versus full year ended 30 June 2025.
**Basis:** Cash NPAT, continuing operations. The comparator is FY25 as restated in the FY26 Profit Announcement. The statutory Consolidated Income Statement shows the same loan impairment expense (LIE) values: $(788)m versus $(726)m (FY26 profit announcement, PDF p.88).
**Overall confidence: 95/100.**

## The movement

LIE increased **$62m, from $726m in FY25 to $788m in FY26**, up 9%.

> "Loan impairment expense was $788 million, an increase of $62 million or 9% on the prior year." (FY26 profit announcement, PDF p.34)

The **loss rate rose 1 basis point, from 7bpts to 8bpts** of average gross loans and acceptances (GLAA).

> "Loan impairment expense as a percentage of average gross loans and acceptances (GLAA) increased 1 basis point to 8 basis points." (FY26 profit announcement, PDF p.34)

Corroboration: the KPI table shows 0.08% vs 0.07% (FY26 profit announcement, PDF p.19, labelled "including discontinued operations"); the presentation shows the loss-rate series 9 / 7 / 8 bpts for FY24 / FY25 / FY26, split consumer 6 / 5 / 6 and corporate 16 / 14 / 12 (FY26 results presentation, PDF p.29); the ASX announcement shows "$788m (Loan loss rate 8bpts)" (PDF p.2). The FY25 profit announcement confirms the FY25 base: $726m and "decreased 2 basis points to 7 basis points" (FY25 profit announcement, PDF p.34).

Half-year phasing: 1H26 $319m, 2H26 $469m (+47% half-on-half). The pressure built in the second half (FY26 profit announcement, PDF p.34).

## Driver attribution — by expense component (primary, exact)

**Residual: $0m.** The three components sum exactly to the +$62m movement.

### 1. Higher net collective provision funding: +$150m (confidence 97)

The collective charge rose from $456m to $606m and more than accounts for the whole movement. The bank names the causes:

> "Loan impairment expense (LIE) increased $62 million, with higher collective provisions mainly reflecting portfolio growth, increased geopolitical risk and macroeconomic uncertainty." (FY26 profit announcement, PDF p.26)

On the balance sheet, corporate collective provisions rose $172m or 7% to $2,797m, "mainly reflecting portfolio growth, increased geopolitical risk and macroeconomic uncertainty", while consumer collective provisions fell $48m or 2% to $2,888m, "reflecting rising house prices over the period and more targeted forward-looking adjustments for higher risk customer cohorts", partly offset by higher arrears and cost-of-living pressures (FY26 profit announcement, PDF p.44). The charge was second-half weighted: $388m in 2H26 versus $218m in 1H26 (PDF p.118).

### 2. Lower net new individually assessed provisioning: -$17m (confidence 96)

New and increased individual provisioning fell from $439m to $422m. Business Banking cites "lower individually assessed provision charges" (FY26 profit announcement, PDF p.69). Consumer individually assessed provisions fell $19m or 16% to $97m on rising house prices; corporate fell $6m to $694m (PDF p.44).

### 3. Higher write-backs of individually assessed provisions: -$71m (confidence 96)

Write-backs rose from $169m to $240m. Business Banking notes "an increase in write-backs" (PDF p.69); Institutional Banking and Markets was "primarily driven by release of individually assessed provisions" (PDF p.73).

### Thematic causes inside the collective charge (unquantified)

The documents do not give dollar values for these causes. They sit inside the +$150m collective driver and are not additive to it.

- **Portfolio growth** (confidence 92). GLAA grew 7% to $1,087,560m (FY26 profit announcement, PDF p.45). The ASX announcement attributes the higher LIE to "portfolio growth and increased geopolitical risk and macroeconomic uncertainty" (PDF p.1). Presentation slide title: "Impairment expense higher reflecting portfolio growth and increased global macroeconomic uncertainty" (PDF p.29).
- **Geopolitical risk and macroeconomic uncertainty** (confidence 92). Named in every segment's FY26 commentary and concentrated in 2H26. Recognised provisions of $6.5bn sit "$2.7 billion above central scenario" expected losses (FY26 results presentation, PDF p.30; ASX announcement, PDF p.2).
- **Cost-of-living pressures and rising consumer arrears** (confidence 90). Home loan 90+ day arrears 0.73% and personal loan arrears 1.72% (ASX announcement, PDF p.2); RBS home loan arrears up 6bpts to 0.75% (FY26 profit announcement, PDF p.65). The bank notes "realised credit losses remained low" (FY26 profit announcement, PDF p.8).

- **RBS +$106m:** "primarily driven by increased geopolitical risk, macroeconomic uncertainty, and rising cost-of-living pressures" (PDF p.34). Note: 2H26 RBS LIE fell $86m on 1H26 as overlays for higher-risk cohorts were released, so the full-year increase came from 1H26 provisioning plus arrears.
- **New Zealand +$11m** (NZD +18m to NZD78m, PDF p.79): "higher collective provisions reflecting deterioration in the unemployment outlook, and increased geopolitical risk and macroeconomic uncertainty", partly offset by lower individually assessed provisions and lower consumer finance write-offs.
- **Business Banking -$45m:** lower individually assessed charges and more write-backs, partly offset by higher collective provisions on portfolio growth and macro uncertainty (PDF p.34).
- **IB&M -$16m:** release of individually assessed provisions, partly offset by higher collective provisions (PDF p.34).
- **Corporate Centre and Other +$6m:** derived from the table values $(5)m to $1m; no narrative is given (PDF p.34).

## Disagreements between sources

1. **FY25 segment comparatives.** The FY25 PA (PDF p.34) shows RBS $274m and Business Banking $353m; the FY26 PA (PDF p.34) restates these to $272m and $355m. The group total is $726m in both. I prefer the FY26 restated figures because the FY26 document states "Comparative information has been restated to conform to presentation in the current period", and the year-on-year deltas ($106m, $45m) are computed on that basis.
2. **Growth percentage.** The PA and ASX announcement say 9%; the presentation (PDF p.24) says 8.5%. The dollar figures are identical and $62m/$726m = 8.54%, so this is rounding precision only.

## Limitations

- No dollar split of the +$150m collective funding increase across portfolio growth, macro/geopolitical overlays, and cost-of-living effects.
- Segment collective/individual splits are qualitative only.
- Average GLAA (the loss-rate denominator) is not disclosed as a dollar figure; loss rates are as stated by the bank.
- The KPI loss-rate table is on an "including discontinued operations" label; both bases give 7bps -> 8bps.
- The 2026 Annual Report notes referenced by the PA (e.g. Note 3.2, impairment methodology) were not among the supplied documents.
