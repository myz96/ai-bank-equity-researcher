# Q: Is Westpac's targeted FY26 productivity enough to restore positive jaws, or is it mainly an offset to continuing wage, banker and UNITE investment pressure? Ground the judgment in the FY25 expense bridge, the investment-spend mix and the disclosed duration/expensing profile of UNITE.

*WBC, periods FY25 — confidence 75/100*

**Answer:** Westpac's FY26 productivity initiatives are not yet enough to restore positive jaws. The FY25 expense bridge shows that investment-spend pressure from UNITE, wage growth, and banker hiring far outstrips the offset from Fit for Growth restructuring savings and occupancy reductions. Productivity is currently a partial drag on jaws, not a cure.

**Reasoning:**

**FY25 Expense Bridge (FY25 vs FY24):** Total operating expenses rose 9% to $11,916m (ev-2). Excluding the one-off $273m Fit for Growth restructuring charge (ev-3), expenses still grew 6%, driven by: staff costs +7% to $6,326m (ev-4) from wage growth, UNITE ramp-up, and investment in bankers (ev-5); technology expenses +13% to $3,136m (ev-21) from UNITE, amortisation, and maintenance (ev-20). These were only partially offset by occupancy -7% to $652m (ev-19) and other expenses -3% to $1,529m (ev-22). Average FTE was flat (+1%), but headcount increases for UNITE and bankers "more than offsetting reductions from productivity initiatives" (ev-6).

**Investment-Spend Mix:** Total investment spend rose 9% to $1,918m (ev-7). UNITE alone accounted for $660m (34% of total), up from $147m in FY24, with 74% expensed (ev-8, ev-9). Of the $1,159m expensed (ev-10), UNITE contributed ~$488m. Growth & productivity received $563m (30%) and risk/regulatory $695m (36%). In Business & Wealth, 42 additional business bankers were hired alongside UNITE step-up (ev-12). In Institutional, expenses rose 12% to $1,647m driven by UNITE ramp-up and front-line staff increases (ev-13).

**UNITE Duration/Expensing Profile:** UNITE is described as resolving structural legacy issues; Discovery phase complete with 8 initiatives delivered (ev-16). Capitalised software has an average amortisation period of 2.7 years (ev-15). The bank states simplification through UNITE is "expected to play a key role in reducing our cost base" (ev-17), but no specific FY26 savings target or duration profile is disclosed.

**Conclusion:** The $273m restructuring charge is a one-off H2FY25 hit, not a recurring saving. With UNITE spend tripling year-on-year, wages rising, and headcount growing to support UNITE and bankers, productivity offsets remain insufficient. Positive jaws require UNITE spend to plateau while savings crystallise — neither condition is met in FY25.

**What the evidence does not settle:** No explicit FY26 productivity savings target or dollar quantification is disclosed. The future expensing/capitalisation split of UNITE beyond FY25 is not specified. Whether UNITE-driven efficiency gains will materialise at scale remains forward-looking.
