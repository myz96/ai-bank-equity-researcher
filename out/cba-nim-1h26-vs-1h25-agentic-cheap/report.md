CBA's net interest margin decreased 4 bps to 2.04% in 1H26 from 2.08% in 1H25 (cash basis). Excluding liquid assets and institutional reverse sale and repurchase agreements, underlying NIM was flat. The decline was driven by higher funding costs (-3 bps) from declining interest rates, unfavourable deposit mix and increased deposit price competition, and lower asset pricing (-2 bps) from home lending and business lending competition, partly offset by +6 bps from the replicating portfolio and equity hedges, +1 bps from favourable portfolio mix from strong at-call deposit growth, and -3 bps from Treasury & Markets due to higher funding costs in Carbon and Commodities and Global Markets and increased reverse repo balances. Liquids dragged -3 bps; basis risk was flat.

## Limitations
- The results book NIM walk includes 'Basis risk' as a separate bar at 0 bps, while the presentation slide combines this into 'Interest rate risk hedging' at +6 bps alongside the replicating portfolio. Both walks sum to -4 bps and agree on all other components.
- Underlying NIM (ex-liquids) was flat per the bank's disclosure, meaning the -4 bps headline decline is entirely attributable to the liquidity drag.
- No statutory-basis NIM figure is separately disclosed for comparison; the KPI table presents only the cash-basis measure.
- Capped at 80: liquids -3 bps. The records these claims cite do not state those numbers, so each one is arithmetic over the evidence rather than a figure read from it.
