# Q: Across CBA, NAB and Westpac in FY25, which bank best converted its disclosed underlying top-line or pre-provision performance into its preferred cash/core earnings measure, and what explains the ordering? Compare direction and drivers rather than absolute dollars, and state the comparability limits of the three non-identical profit measures.

*CBA, NAB, WBC, periods FY25 — confidence 85/100*

**CBA converted best.** Its cash NPAT rose 4% to $10,252m on 3% operating-performance growth — the only one of the three whose preferred measure grew. NAB's cash earnings fell 0.2% despite underlying profit rising 1.3%, and WBC's net profit ex-Notable Items fell 2% on a 3% pre-provision decline. Ordering on conversion: **CBA > WBC > NAB** — WBC's positive gap came from an impairment tailwind, NAB's negative gap from expenses and impairments outrunning revenue.

**CBA** (cash basis, FY ended 30 Jun 25): operating income +5% to $28,465m; operating performance (its pre-provision line) +3% to $15,469m; cash NPAT +$416m or +4% to $10,252m. Drivers: NII +5% on a 9bp NIM rise (2bps excluding liquid assets and institutional pooled facilities), expenses +6% on an underlying basis excluding remediation ($52m domestic, $33m NZ) and Bankwest restructuring ($45m FY25 vs $89m FY24) notables, and loan impairment expense down $76m. Statutory NPAT rose 8% to $10,116m.

**NAB** (cash earnings basis, FY ended 30 Sep 25): revenue +2.9% (only +1.4% ex-Markets & Treasury); expenses +4.6% including $130m payroll review/remediation (+3.2% ex); credit impairment charge up 14.4% to $833m. Underlying profit rose 1.3% to $10,965m, but cash earnings fell 0.2% to $7,091m — the only bank whose preferred measure fell despite pre-provision growth. Statutory NPAT fell 2.9% to $6,759m as non-cash items widened to −$303m (from −$39m).

**WBC** (ex-Notable Items, FY ended 30 Sep 25): net operating income +3% to $22,464m; expenses +9% including a $273m Fit for Growth restructuring charge (+6% ex); pre-provision profit fell 3% to $10,548m. Impairment charges fell 21% to $424m (5bps vs 7bps of average loans), cushioning net profit ex-Notables to −2% at $6,972m; statutory fell 1% to $6,916m.

**Comparability limits:** the three measures are not identical. CBA's cash NPAT excludes only non-cash items (disposal gains/losses, hedging/IFRS volatility). NAB's cash earnings also excludes acquired-intangible amortisation and acquisition/disposal/closure costs. WBC's measure is "comparable to a 'cash earnings' basis" but excludes only disclosed Notable Items (FY25: hedging, −$56m post tax) — it does not strip intangible amortisation. All are non-statutory, unaudited management measures. Periods differ (CBA June vs NAB/WBC September year-ends), NAB restated FY24 comparatives, and statutory NPAT moved differently (CBA +8%, NAB −2.9%, WBC −1%), so the ranking is measure-specific.

## Limitations
- The 'conversion' gaps are computed from the banks' printed growth rates; none of the banks prints a conversion metric itself.
- Periods are not identical: CBA's FY ended 30 June 2025; NAB's and WBC's FY ended 30 September 2025.
- The pre-provision lines are not identically defined: CBA's 'operating performance' is cash basis with remediation/Bankwest notables excluded from expenses; NAB's 'underlying profit' is cash basis including remediation; WBC's 'pre-provision profit' is ex-Notable Items.

- The in-scope investor presentations and discussion packs were not used; the results documents are the higher-authority source for these figures.
- The ranking is specific to each bank's preferred non-statutory measure; on statutory NPAT the ordering differs (CBA +8%, WBC −1%, NAB −2.9%).
