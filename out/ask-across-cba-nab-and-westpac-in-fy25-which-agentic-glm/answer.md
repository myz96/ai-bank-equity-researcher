## Limitations
- The banks print no 'conversion' metric; the ranking is derived from each bank's own printed growth rates, which sit on non-identical measure definitions (CBA cash NPAT vs NAB cash earnings vs WBC net profit ex Notable Items).
- Fiscal periods differ: CBA's FY ended 30 June 2025; NAB's and WBC's FY ended 30 September 2025 — a three-month timing difference in the rate and credit environment.
- NAB restated FY24 comparatives for an expense-presentation change, so its growth rates are on a restated basis.
- Bank-defined 'underlying' adjustments (CBA NIM +2bps ex-mix; NAB revenue +1.4% ex-M&T; WBC expenses +6% ex-restructuring) are each bank's own qualifications, not common adjustments.
- Absolute dollars were not compared per the question; the three banks differ in scale (cash NPAT $10.3bn CBA vs $7.1bn NAB vs $7.0bn WBC), so equal percentage moves are unequal dollar amounts.
- All three preferred measures are non-IFRS/non-AAS and unaudited; statutory results differ (e.g. CBA statutory NPAT $10,116m, NAB statutory net profit $6,759m, WBC statutory net profit $6,916m).
