# Q: Assess whether NAB's FY25 Business & Private Banking growth was value-accretive rather than growth bought at the expense of margin or credit quality. Reconcile lending, revenue and cash-earnings growth with the NIM, impairment-charge and sector/NPL disclosures, and identify what is broad-based versus single-name driven.

*NAB, periods FY25 — confidence 88/100*

**Verdict: broadly value-accretive on returns, but the quality of the growth is mixed — margin gave ground and 2H25 asset-quality deterioration, while partly single-name, was concentrated in the very portfolio NAB is growing.**

**Reconciliation (FY25 v FY24, cash basis, restated comparatives).** B&PB grew gross loans and acceptances 6.8% to $279.5bn (business lending +7.3% to $166.3bn; housing +5.8% to $109.3bn) and deposits +6.6% to $237.9bn. Yet net operating income rose only 2.1% to $8,531m (NII +2.8% to $7,499m) and cash earnings just 1.6% to $3,330m — so revenue grew at roughly a quarter of the pace of lending. The gap is the NIM: down 8bps to 3.01% on "competitive pressures impacting both the business and housing lending portfolios, as well as higher deposit costs including mix impacts due to term deposit growth," partly offset by deposit/capital replicating portfolio earnings. Operating expenses rose 2.9%, lifting the cost-to-income ratio 30bps to 37.9%.

**Margin vs credit quality.** The margin give-back was not redeemed by a falling impairment charge in absolute terms — it rose $6m (+1.1%) to $529m, and 2H25 jumped 23.2% HoH to $292m on "higher business lending charges driven by higher levels of impairment, asset quality deterioration and volume growth." But relative to the larger book the charge-to-GLAs ratio actually fell 1bp to 0.19%, so credit cost did not erode value at the margin. Returns were flat, not accretive: cash earnings on average RWA slipped 1bp to 2.18% while RWAs grew 9.0% — faster than lending — "mostly due to growth in business lending volumes, combined with deterioration in asset quality."

**Broad-based vs single-name.** Group NPLs rose 18.2% to $12,094m, "mainly driven by deterioration in the Business and Private Banking business lending portfolio"; impaired assets rose 53.9% and impaired assets/GLAs 10bps to 0.49% for the division. However, the presentation isolates single-name risk: the B&PB business lending NPL ratio rose from 2.66% (Sep 24) to 3.22% (Sep 25), but "excluding two large well secured Agri customers, underlying NPL ratio down 3bps to 3.01%" (those two customers contribute 21bps), with "stable to improving NPL outcomes in 2H25 for most sectors." So the ratio deterioration is substantially two well-secured Agri names; the growth itself (business lending +6.7%, deposits +6.6%) is broad-based.

**Net:** volume growth is real and diversified and credit cost per unit of lending is stable, but flat RWA returns and an 8bps NIM decline mean the growth was not clearly value-accretive — more margin-neutral volume bought at stable, slightly deteriorating credit quality.

**What the evidence does not settle:** whether individual pricing on new business lending was accretive (NAB discloses portfolio NIM only, no loan-level ROE), the identity/loss content of the two Agri customers, and the post-restatement comparability of FY24 NIM (portfolios were transferred between divisions).

## Limitations
- NAB does not disclose loan-level or new-business pricing, so 'value-accretive' can only be assessed on divisional returns (cash earnings on average RWA), not incremental deal economics.
- FY24 comparatives were restated for portfolio transfers between Personal Banking and B&PB and for an updated expense presentation, so pre-restatement FY24 NIM/earnings are not directly comparable.
- The two large well secured Agri customers are not named, and their provisioning/loss content is not disclosed; 'well secured' implies low expected loss but is NAB's characterisation.
- Cash earnings is a non-IFRS, unaudited measure by NAB's own definition; statutory divisional profit is not disclosed.
- 2H25 impairment step-up partially reflects lower home lending charges in 1H25 (house price movements, lower arrears), so the HoH comparison overstates the business-lending-only trend.
