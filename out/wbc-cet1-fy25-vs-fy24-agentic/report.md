# WBC — cet1 — FY25 vs FY24

**Movement (ex_notables basis):** 1249bps → 1253bps (+4bps) | **Attribution confidence:** 58/100

*Read from: row 'CET1 capital ratio (Level 2)', column As at 30 Sept 2024 -> column As at 30 Sept 2025*

Westpac's APRA Level 2 CET1 ratio was 12.53% at 30 Sep 2025 against 12.49% at 30 Sep 2024 — up just 4 bps (ev-1, ev-35). The bank's own FY-on-FY explanation: "The increase from net profit less the payment of 2024 final ordinary dividend and 2025 interim ordinary dividend, other reserve movements and lower capitalised software balances was offset by higher Total RWA and on market share buybacks" (ev-2, ev-22). CET1 capital rose $1,732m to $56,380m, but RWA rose to $450.0bn from $437.4bn (+3%), driven overwhelmingly by IRRBB RWA up 33% to $37.3bn (ev-4, ev-21, ev-18). Statutory net profit was $6,916m, down 1% (ev-24); ordinary DPS rose to 153c and no special dividend repeated (ev-1 context). Westpac publishes NO full-year CET1 walk — only a Mar-25→Sep-25 walk (+29 bps: net profit 80, 1H25 dividend (58), RWA (12), IRRBB 5, Other 16, capital return (2)) (ev-6), so bar-level FY attribution is not disclosed. Level 1 CET1 12.74% (+5 bps) and internationally comparable 18.28% (+1 bp) are different measures (ev-5).

## Limitations
- Westpac publishes NO Sep-24 to Sep-25 CET1 walk in either document. Only the Mar-25 to Sep-25 walk is charted (results announcement p29; investor pack p31 and p77), so no bar-level FY25-vs-FY24 attribution exists in the bank's own disclosure. Earnings, dividend, deductions and buyback drivers are therefore left unquantified.
- The (35) bps total-RWA contribution is MY arithmetic: FY24 CET1 capital of $54,648m divided by FY25 RWA of $450,048m against FY24 RWA of $437,430m. Westpac does not print an FY-on-FY bps figure for RWA, so this is capped in confidence and the capital-generation side is left unquantified rather than derived to force a sum.
- Residual of roughly +39 bps is the unquantified net of earnings, dividends, other reserve/software deductions and buybacks. It is not a published figure; it is the difference between the +4 bps movement and my computed (35) bps RWA drag.
- The FY-on-FY IRRBB RWA increase of $9.3bn largely REVERSES after the balance date: the revised APS 117 effective 1 October 2025 cuts RWA by $14bn, shown as a +39 bps CET1 adjustment 'post 30 Sep 25'. The reported 12.53% therefore understates the run-rate ratio on the new standard.
- The remaining on-market buyback previously announced in Nov-23, May-24 and Nov-24 is disclosed as a further (23) bps under 'Other considerations', i.e. NOT yet reflected in the 12.53%. Only $0.1bn of buyback ((2) bps) was executed in the second half.
- The FY25 total dollar value of buybacks executed during the year, and the FY-on-FY DRP neutralisation amount, are not disclosed in either document, so the capital-returns driver cannot be sized for the task window.
- No divestment, acquisition, capital-floor or model-change bps impact is disclosed inside the FY24-to-FY25 window; the pack notes the standardised floor is 'met' but gives no bps. These taxonomy drivers are therefore absent rather than zero.
- Both documents in the corpus are the same FY25 results release, so corroboration is within one reporting event rather than genuinely independent; no FY24 book was available to check restatement of the 12.49% comparative.
- Basis normalised from 'cash' to 'ex_notables': no page in evidence prints 'cash' beside the movement, and the registry names ex_notables as the bank's headline basis.
- No published walk covers FY25 vs FY24: the bank's walk for this metric describes another comparison, so the driver split is not walk-verified for this comparison. Confidence is capped at 85.
