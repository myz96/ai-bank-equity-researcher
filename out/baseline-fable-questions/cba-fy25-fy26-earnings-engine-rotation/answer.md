# CBA: FY25 to FY26 — the earnings engine rotated from margin to volume

**Verdict.** FY26's faster cash-NPAT growth (+7% vs +4% in FY25) was higher quality. The engine rotated from margin-led to volume-led, jaws turned positive, and the result absorbed a rising impairment charge instead of relying on a falling one. Caveats: a +5bp rate-hedge tailwind inside the margin, one-off items in other income, and a lower provision-coverage ratio.

**The mix** (cash basis, continuing operations; FY26 PA p16, FY25 PA p16):

| | FY25 (vs FY24) | FY26 (vs FY25) |
|---|---|---|
| Net interest income | $24,023m, +5% | $25,586m, +7% |
| Other operating income | $4,442m, +2% | $4,638m, +4% |
| Operating expenses | $(12,996)m, +6% | $(13,755)m, +6% |
| Pre-provision profit | $15,469m, +3% | $16,469m, +6% |
| Loan impairment expense | $(726)m, −9% | $(788)m, +9% |
| Cash NPAT | $10,252m, +4% | $10,982m, +7% |
| Avg interest-earning assets | $1,154bn, +1% | $1,246bn, +8% |
| NIM | 2.08%, +9bp | 2.05%, −3bp |
| Loan-loss rate (bp of avg GLAA) | 7 | 8 |

**Rotation.** FY25 NII growth came from margin: NIM +9bp on AIEA of only +1%. CBA itself flagged 7bp of that as composition (lower liquids and pooled facilities, NII-neutral); the underlying +2bp was Capital/Replicating +9bp minus funding costs −7bp from deposit competition. FY25 NPAT also had a −9% impairment tailwind. FY26 reversed the pattern: NII +7% was driven by AIEA +$92bn (+8%; lending +7%, business loans +10%), with NIM −3bp, and impairment became a +9% headwind. Pre-provision profit growth doubled (+3% to +6%).

**FY26 margin bridge.** The −3bp (2.08%→2.05%) reconciles as: liquids −3bp (NII-neutral); asset pricing −5bp (home −4, business/institutional −1, competition); funding costs flat; portfolio mix +2bp; Capital/Replicating/Other +5bp (replicating +4, capital hedges +1); Treasury & Markets −2bp. So underlying NIM was stable, and reported compression is composition, not franchise erosion. The presentation groups the bars slightly differently (Liquids & repos −4bp, T&M −1bp; liquids +$15bn, repos +$11bn) — same −3bp total; I prefer the PA's fuller split. Competitive pressure migrated from the liability side (FY25 funding costs −7bp) to the asset side (FY26 asset pricing −5bp), each year offset by rate-hedge earnings — a cyclical, not franchise, offset.

**Quality read.** Supporting higher quality: growth at/above system in all five core domestic categories; underlying cost-to-income fell 30bp to 44.9% (headline CTI 45.5%, −20bp); ROE +50bp to 14.0%; and the loan-loss rate at 8bp remains below FY23 (12bp) and FY24 (9bp), with realised losses low, NPEs down 4bp to 0.69% of TCE, and the rise driven by collective provisions for geopolitical/macro uncertainty and portfolio growth. Against it: provision coverage fell from 1.60% to 1.53% of credit RWA, 2H26 impairment annualised at 9bp with consumer arrears rising (home 0.73%, personal 1.72%), and OOI included a CommInsure milestone payment and a Gemini IPO fair-value gain.

**Limitations.** CBA does not quantify the NII dollar-split between volume and margin, the dollar size of the OOI one-offs, or the collective-vs-individually-assessed split of the $62m LIE increase; FY25 comparatives were restated by CBA.

**Confidence: 88/100.**
