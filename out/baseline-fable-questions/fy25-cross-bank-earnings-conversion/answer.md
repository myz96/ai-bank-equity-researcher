# FY25: who converted pre-provision performance into their preferred earnings measure best?

**Verdict: CBA converted best. WBC's conversion was positive but only cushioned a decline. NAB converted worst.** The ordering is set almost entirely by the direction of the credit impairment line, because all three banks ran negative jaws (expense growth above income growth).

| Bank (preferred measure, year-end) | Income | Expenses | Pre-provision line | Impairment line | Preferred measure | Conversion gap* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CBA (cash NPAT, cont. ops; 30 Jun 25) | +5% | +6% | "Operating performance" +3% ($15,469m) | LIE −9% ($726m) | +4% ($10,252m) | +0.8pp |
| NAB (cash earnings; 30 Sep 25) | +2.9% | +4.6% | "Underlying profit" +1.3% ($10,965m) | CIC +14.4% ($833m) | −0.2% ($7,091m) | −1.5pp |
| WBC (NPAT ex-Notable Items; 30 Sep 25) | +3% | +9% | "Pre-provision profit" −3% ($10,548m) | Charges −21% ($424m) | −2% ($6,972m) | +0.5pp |

\*Preferred-measure growth minus pre-provision growth, computed from the disclosed $m; all % movements are as printed.

## The ordering, explained

1. **CBA — best.** It is the only bank where the preferred measure grew faster than the pre-provision line, and both grew. A 9% fall in loan impairment expense (to $726m) added ~0.8pp on top of +3% operating performance, with the effective tax rate flat at 30.5%. The credit line amplified genuine growth.
2. **WBC — positive conversion, poor content.** A 21% fall in impairment charges (5bps of average gross loans vs 7bps) converted a −3% pre-provision result into a −2% profit result. The uplift is real, but it only cushioned a decline that WBC itself created: 9% expense growth, including a $273m Fit for Growth restructuring charge, against 3% income growth.
3. **NAB — worst.** NAB posted the "cleanest" positive pre-provision story after CBA (underlying profit +1.3%), yet cash earnings fell 0.2%. It was the only major with rising credit costs: the CIC rose 14.4% to $833m, driven by $964m of individually assessed charges (business lending and unsecured retail), net of a $131m collective release. Non-controlling interests also doubled to $39m.

So the driver ranking is: credit tailwind reinforcing growth (CBA) > credit tailwind offsetting an expense blow-out (WBC) > credit headwind plus NCI drag erasing growth (NAB).

## Comparability limits

- **The three measures are not the same construct.** CBA's cash NPAT excludes hedging/IFRS volatility and business acquisition/disposal items; NAB's cash earnings also excludes amortisation of acquired intangibles and is stated after NCI; WBC's ex-Notables measure excluded only hedging items in FY25 (Large items nil), so it is the least adjusted.
- **Each keeps different one-offs inside the "underlying" result**: WBC's $273m restructuring, NAB's $130m payroll review/remediation, CBA's ~$130m remediation and Bankwest restructuring provisions.
- **Different year-ends**: CBA to 30 June 2025; NAB and WBC to 30 September 2025 — different rate and credit windows.
- **Restated comparatives** (NAB expense re-presentation; CBA restatements) mean growth rates sit on each bank's own restated base; the disclosures do not permit a like-for-like reconstruction.

**Confidence: 82/100.**
