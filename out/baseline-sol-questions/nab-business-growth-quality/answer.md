# NAB FY25 Business & Private Banking growth quality

**Judgment: value-accretive in dollars, not in unit economics.** Revenue and earnings rose and loss-charge intensity eased, but earnings lagged lending, NIM fell and return on assets declined. This is not a clean quality-growth verdict.

Business lending rose 7.3% to $166.3bn, housing 5.8% to $109.3bn and GLAs 6.8% to $279.5bn. (NAB-FY25-results-book.pdf, PDF page 36, “Volumes ($bn) Housing lending 109.3 103.3 5.8 109.3 106.0 3.1 Business lending 166.3 155.0 7.3”; “Gross loans and acceptances 279.5 261.7 6.8 279.5 267.4 4.5”) Net operating income grew 2.1% to $8,531m, while underlying profit and cash earnings each rose 1.6%, to $5,299m and $3,330m. (NAB-FY25-results-book.pdf, PDF page 36, “Net operating income 8,531 8,358 2.1 4,354 4,177 4.2”; “Underlying profit 5,299 5,217 1.6 2,703 2,596 4.1”; “Cash earnings 3,330 3,277 1.6 1,683 1,647 2.2”)

Pricing is the clearest qualification. NIM fell 8 bps to 3.01%, and cash earnings/average assets fell 5 bps to 1.25%. (NAB-FY25-results-book.pdf, PDF page 36, “Cash earnings on average assets 1.25 1.30 (5 bps) 1.24 1.26 (2 bps)”; “Net interest margin 3.01 3.09 (8 bps) 3.02 3.01 1 bp”) NAB attributes this to competitive lending pressure and higher deposit costs. (NAB-FY25-results-book.pdf, PDF page 37, “Net interest margin decreased by 8 basis points reflecting competitive pressures impacting both the business and housing lending portfolios, as well as higher deposit costs including mix impacts due to term deposit growth.”)

Credit was mixed. FY25 CIC was $529m versus $523m, but CIC/GLAs improved 1 bp to 0.19%. (NAB-FY25-results-book.pdf, PDF page 36, “Credit impairment charge (529) (523) 1.1 (292) (237) 23.2”; “Credit impairment charge to GLAs - annualised 0.19 0.20 (1 bp) 0.21 0.18 3 bps”) Yet 2H25 CIC rose to $292m/0.21% from $237m/0.18% in 1H25, and NPEs increased from $5,580m/2.12% to $7,079m/2.54%. (NAB-FY25-investor-presentation.pdf, PDF page 49, “0.16% 0.25% 0.18% 0.21% Credit impairment charge to GLAs - annualised 201 322 237 292 1H24 2H24 1H25 2H25”); (NAB-FY25-investor-presentation.pdf, PDF page 95, “4,588 5,580 6,485 7,079 1.79% 2.12% 2.41% 2.54% Mar 24 Sep 24 Mar 25 Sep 25”)

The **growth was broad-based**: Agri, Health, CRE and Other grew 3.5%, 5.9%, 7.4% and 10.4%, including Trade, Manufacturing, Construction and Transport. (NAB-FY25-investor-presentation.pdf, PDF page 50, “3.5% 5.9% 7.4% 10.4% 7.3% 9.2% Agri Health CRE2 Other NAB B&PB3 NAB Small business3”; “Broad based growth across most sectors including Trade, Manufacturing, Construction, Transport”) By contrast, September-versus-March B&PB default-but-not-impaired deterioration was mainly **single-name driven**. (NAB-FY25-investor-presentation.pdf, PDF page 87, “Deterioration Sep 25 v Mar 25 mainly reflects single names impacting impaired ratio (C&IB, NZB) and default but not impaired ratio (B&PB)”) Agriculture’s 2H25 NPL uplift mainly reflected two large, well-secured customers. (NAB-FY25-investor-presentation.pdf, PDF page 97, “Uplift in NPL ratio during 2H25 mainly due to customer specific issues for two large well secured customers”)

**Limitations:** The growth categories are customer segments, not industries. NAB omits new-business spreads, sector margins, cohort losses and B&PB economic-profit/ROE hurdle data. It does not quantify the single-name share of the full-year NPE increase; agriculture’s attribution is industry-wide. Transfers limit comparability.

**Confidence: 88/100.**
