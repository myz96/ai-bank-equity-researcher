# Westpac FY25: caution remains

**Judgment: No.** FY25 shows normalisation in the *charge flow*, but September's provision stock still embeds material caution.

The FY25 charge fell to $424m/5bp from $537m/7bp; 2H25 fell to $174m/4bp from $250m/6bp (WBC-FY25-results-announcement.pdf, PDF page 21, “The credit impairment charge of $424 million represented 5 basis points of average loans, down from 7 basis points in the prior year.”; “The credit impairment charge of $174 million represented 4 basis points of average loans, down from 6 basis points in the prior period.”). Offsets kept flow low despite new losses: $408m new IAPs less $247m recoveries and $195m write-backs produced a $34m benefit; the $458m CAP charge was $561m write-offs less a $103m other-CAP benefit (WBC-FY25-results-announcement.pdf, PDF page 21, “The IAP benefit of $34 million comprised: • New IAPs of $408 million”; “Recoveries of $247 million”; “Write-backs of $195 million”; “The CAP charge of $458 million comprised write-offs of $561 million which was partly offset by a benefit in other changes in CAP of $103 million.”).

One inconsistency: the 2H table's +$81m IAP result reconciles $157m new IAPs against $132m recoveries and $106m write-backs, but prose calls it an “IAP charge” (WBC-FY25-results-announcement.pdf, PDF page 21, “New IAPs (157)”; “Write-backs 106”; “Recoveries 132”; “Total IAPs, write-backs and recoveries 81”; “The IAP charge of $81 million comprised”). I prefer the table: an $81m benefit plus $255m CAP charge equals the $174m total charge.

The stock declined only 2% to $4,987m. On loans and commitments, $4,978m comprised $4,439m CAP ($4,201m modelled plus $238m overlays) and $539m IAP; debt-security ECL of $3m and $6m brings total ECL to $4,987m (WBC-FY25-results-announcement.pdf, PDF page 24, “Modelled provision 4,201”; “Overlays 238”; “Total collectively assessed provisions 4,439”; “Individually assessed provisions 539”; “Total provision for ECL on loans and credit commitments 4,978”; “Total provision for ECL 4,987”). In 2H, modelled CAP fell $120m but overlays rose $108m, leaving total CAP just $12m lower; IAP fell $72m, while the downside weight rose another 2.5ppt (WBC-FY25-presentation-and-IDP.pdf, PDF page 29, “CAP $12m lower”; “Overlays increased $108m”; “2.5ppt increase in downside scenario weight”; “IAP decreased $72m due to a single name write-off”).

That caution is material: reported probability-weighted ECL was $4,987m versus $3,040m under 100% base and $7,152m under 100% downside—explicitly $1.9bn above base (WBC-FY25-presentation-and-IDP.pdf, PDF page 54, “$1.9bn in provisions above the base case ECL”; “Reported probability-weighted ECL 4,987”; “100% base case ECL 3,040”; “100% downside ECL 7,152”). Weights were 2.5% upside, 50.0% base and 47.5% downside (WBC-FY25-results-announcement.pdf, PDF page 24, “Upside 2.5”; “Base 50.0”; “Downside 47.5”). Downside severity assumes GDP (6%), unemployment 11%, residential prices (27%) and commercial prices (32%) (WBC-FY25-presentation-and-IDP.pdf, PDF page 54, “GDP growth (6%)”; “Unemployment 11%”; “Residential property prices (27%)”; “Commercial property prices (32%)”).

**Conclusion:** recoveries and benign realised credit explain the low charge; overlays and a near-half downside weight show normalisation was not complete.

**Limitations:** no 100% upside ECL, weighting sensitivities, overlay allocation or full scenario paths are disclosed; the weighted figure cannot be rebuilt or future adequacy inferred.

**Confidence: 94/100.**
