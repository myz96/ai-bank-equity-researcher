# FY25 earnings conversion: CBA leads

## Judgment

On strict **pre-provision-to-preferred-earnings conversion**, the ordering is **CBA first, Westpac second, NAB third**:

- **CBA:** operating performance rose 3% and cash NPAT rose 4%. (CBA-FY25-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF page 26, “from continuing operations increased $416 million or 4% on the prior year to $10,252 million.”) (CBA-FY25-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF page 8, “Pre-provision profit $15,469m 3% on FY24”)
- **Westpac:** pre-provision profit fell 3%, but net profit excluding Notable Items fell a lesser 2%. (WBC-FY25-presentation-and-IDP.pdf, PDF page 20, “Pre-provision profit $10.8bn $10.5bn (3%)”) (WBC-FY25-results-announcement.pdf, PDF page 9, “Net profit excluding Notable Items was $6,972 million, a decrease of 2%”)
- **NAB:** underlying profit rose 1.3%, yet cash earnings fell 0.2%. (NAB-FY25-results-book.pdf, PDF page 5, “Underlying profit rose 1.3% over FY25, benefitting from good volume growth and stronger M&T income.”) (NAB-FY25-results-book.pdf, PDF page 4, “Cash earnings(i) Down 0.2% v FY24”)

Terminal earnings growth instead ranks **CBA, NAB, Westpac**.

## Drivers

**CBA:** operating income grew 5% versus expenses at 6%, while a $76 million impairment decrease lifted cash growth above pre-provision growth. (CBA-FY25-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF page 26, “The result was driven by a 3% increase in operating performance with a 5% increase in operating income and a 6% increase in operating expenses 2”) (CBA-FY25-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF page 26, “Loan impairment expense (LIE) decreased $76 million, reflecting an improving base case economic outlook and lower loss experience”)

**Westpac:** 3% revenue growth was overwhelmed by 9% expense growth. (WBC-FY25-presentation-and-IDP.pdf, PDF page 20, “Revenue $21.8bn $22.5bn 3% Expenses ($10.9bn) ($11.9bn) 9% Expenses excluding restructuring charge ($10.9bn) ($11.6bn) 6%”) Excluding restructuring, expenses still rose 6% on staff costs and UNITE investment; lower impairments cushioned earnings. (WBC-FY25-results-announcement.pdf, PDF page 9, “Credit impairment charges of $424 million represented 5 basis points of average gross loans compared to 7 basis points of average gross loans in the prior year.”)

**NAB:** revenue rose 2.9% versus expenses at 4.6%, including payroll remediation; impairment then rose to $833 million from $728 million, erasing the remaining growth. (NAB-FY25-results-book.pdf, PDF page 5, “Revenue increased by 2.9%”) (NAB-FY25-results-book.pdf, PDF page 5, “Expenses increased by 4.6% including $130 million related to payroll review and remediation charges.”) (NAB-FY25-results-book.pdf, PDF page 5, “Credit impairment charge was $833 million, versus a FY24 charge of $728 million.”)

## Comparability limits

This is directional, not standardized. CBA cash NPAT is its preferred profit before specified non-cash items; NAB adjusts continuing-operations statutory profit for its own list; Westpac says ex-Notable-Items measures are only comparable to cash earnings. (CBA-FY25-profit-announcement.pdf, PDF page 148, “This is management’s preferred measure of the Group’s financial performance.”) (NAB-FY25-results-book.pdf, PDF page 12, “Cash earnings is calculated by adjusting statutory net profit from continuing operations for certain non-cash earnings items.”) (WBC-FY25-results-announcement.pdf, PDF page 2, “Income statement measures excluding Notable Items which is comparable to a 'cash earnings' basis”) Starting measures, exclusions and year-ends differ; Westpac’s result includes restructuring. No harmonized ratio is disclosed.

**Confidence: 94/100.**
