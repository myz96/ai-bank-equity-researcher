# Q: Assess whether NAB's FY25 Business & Private Banking growth was value-accretive rather than growth bought at the expense of margin or credit quality. Reconcile lending, revenue and cash-earnings growth with the NIM, impairment-charge and sector/NPL disclosures, and identify what is broad-based versus single-name driven.

*NAB, periods FY25 — confidence 80/100*

## Direct answer

**Only partly.** FY25 B&PB growth was volume-led and broad-based, but on NAB's own printed figures it was *not* value-accretive at the margin: GLAs grew 6.8% and RWAs 9.0%, yet net operating income grew only 2.1% and cash earnings 1.6%. NIM fell 8bps and returns on capital deployed slipped (cash earnings/avg RWA 2.18% vs 2.19%). Credit quality is the more benign leg: the FY25 charge was near-flat and the *ratio* improved, and the headline NPL jump is materially single-name (Agri) driven. So: margin was conceded, credit quality was not obviously bought — but capital intensity outran earnings.

## Reconciliation

**Lending vs revenue vs earnings (all cash, FY25 v restated FY24):** business lending +7.3% to $166.3bn, housing +5.8%, GLAs +6.8% to $279.5bn [ev-6,7,8]; AIEA +5.4% [ev-16]. But NII rose only 2.8% ($7,499m) [ev-1], net operating income 2.1% [ev-2] and cash earnings 1.6% (+$53m) [ev-5,ev-18]. Revenue grew at roughly a third of volume growth — the volume/revenue wedge *is* the margin concession.

**NIM:** 3.01% vs 3.09%, −8bps, "reflecting competitive pressures impacting both the business and housing lending portfolios, as well as higher deposit costs including mix impacts due to term deposit growth" [ev-10,ev-19]. 2H25 stabilised at 3.02% (+1bp) [ev-42]. Group NIM rose 3bps [ev-50], so the compression is division-specific.

**Capital:** RWAs +9.0% to $159.2bn — "growth in business lending volumes, combined with deterioration in asset quality" [ev-9,ev-21]. Cash earnings/avg RWA −1bp FY, −7bps HoH [ev-11]. Cost-to-income 37.9% vs 37.6% [ev-12], with $102m/3.2% underlying expense growth on bankers and technology [ev-23].

**Credit — better than the NPL optics:** charge $529m, +1.1%; charge/GLAs 0.19% vs 0.20%, i.e. *down* 1bp [ev-4,ev-15]. FY increase "mainly reflects a higher charge for home lending, partially offset by a lower charge for business lending" [ev-20]. 2H25 was worse (+23.2% to $292m; 0.21%) on business lending impairment and volume [ev-4,ev-22,ev-43].

**Broad-based vs single-name:** broad-based — growth spread across Agri +3.5%, Health +5.9%, CRE +7.4%, Other +10.4%, Small business +9.2% [ev-37], "Broad based growth across most sectors" [ev-38], with share gains (APRA 21.7% vs 21.5%; RBA SME 28.0% vs 27.7%) [ev-39,40]. Single-name — B&PB business-lending NPLs 3.22% of GLAs vs 2.66% [ev-27], but this "includes 21bps relating to 2 large Agri customers" and "excluding two large well secured Agri customers, underlying NPL ratio down 3bps to 3.01%" [ev-24,ev-28]. Group DBNI deterioration is attributed to "a small number of large customers in the Agriculture portfolio in B&PB" [ev-30,ev-52]. Security held up: 73% fully secured, 6% unsecured [ev-33]; PD≥2.5% balances 14%, flat HoH [ev-32].

## Not settled
Divisional NPLs still rose 56bps gross [ev-27]; the 18% ROE is **B&PB and C&IB combined**, on a 11.25% CET1 assumption — not a standalone B&PB return [ev-53,54].

## Limitations
- No standalone B&PB ROE or economic-profit disclosure is printed; the 18% ROE covers B&PB and C&IB combined on a 11.25% CET1 assumption excluding centrally held RWA, so 'value-accretive' cannot be tested against a divisional cost of capital.
- The documents do not quantify the dollar size of the two large Agri exposures, only their 21bps effect on the B&PB business-lending NPL ratio.
- No B&PB-specific NIM waterfall with sized bars was located; the 8bps decline is explained only in narrative terms (lending competition, deposit costs/mix, replicating portfolios), so the split between price competition and deposit mix is not quantified.
- FY24 B&PB comparatives are restated twice over (portfolio transfers with Personal Banking, and an expense presentation change), so the 1.6% cash earnings and 2.1% revenue growth rates sit on a restated base and are not like-for-like with originally published FY24 figures.
- Sector growth rates on presentation p.50 are on a customer-segment basis, not industry, with Agri/Health/Other affected by segmentation refinements — so sector-level breadth is not strictly comparable to the ANZSIC-based NPL sector chart.
- B&PB divisional credit impairment charge is not split between individually assessed and collective in the divisional tables, so the single-name share of the B&PB charge itself (as opposed to the NPL ratio) cannot be isolated.
- The PD>=2.5% higher-risk balance series changed basis at Mar 25 to align with Pillar 3 Table CR6 and prior periods were not restated, so the 14% is not comparable with the earlier 20-24% readings.
- No third document (e.g. Pillar 3 or Annual Report) was read to corroborate; figures rest on the FY25 results book and investor presentation only.
